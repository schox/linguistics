# Why a script stays unread

This vault has spent most of its length arguing one answer: **corpus size**. Below some quantity of text a proposed reading can be neither proved nor disproved, so no method wins, because the evidence does not determine an answer. That is [the evidentiary threshold](evidentiary-threshold-and-unicity-distance.md), and it is Barber and Shannon reaching the same result independently.

Then the vault wrote up four scripts that *were* read, and the explanation did not fit any of them. Not one of the four solved decipherments was blocked by a shortage of text.

This note is the correction. There are at least two families of obstacle, they need different responses, and conflating them has been the vault's own error.

## Family one: the evidence does not decide

The corpus is too small, or too short, or too fragmentary, for any reading to be distinguished from its rivals. This is the case the vault has treated as standard, and it is real.

- The [Phaistos Disc](phaistos-disc.md) is the limit: 241 sign-impressions on one object, 45 distinct signs. Del Freo's judgment is that no proposed decipherment can be verified "in the absence of similar inscriptions". That is not a statement about anyone's cleverness.
- [Linear A](linear-a.md) has about 31 percent of [Linear B](linear-b.md)'s documents and 13 percent of its text, because its documents average around five signs against Linear B's twelve. Short texts are worse than proportionately unhelpful.
- [Cypro-Minoan](cypro-minoan.md) is 243 short inscriptions, or three corpora of a few dozen each depending on a classification decision nobody has settled.

The distinguishing feature of this family is that **the bound is computable in principle before you start**. You can ask how much text there is and how much a decision procedure would need. That is what makes it a genuine epistemic limit and not a complaint.

**This framing is refined later in the note.** Applying it to the seven unread scripts showed that family one is not really about quantity at all, and that a corpus can fail to determine an answer while being perfectly large. The sections below are the argument as first stated; the diagnosis is where it gets corrected.

## Family two: everything else

The corpus was sufficient and the script stayed unread anyway. Phase 2 produced four distinct mechanisms.

**A wrong theory.** [Egyptian hieroglyphs](egyptian-hieroglyphs.md) went unread for roughly fourteen centuries with an abundant corpus, a readable descendant language in Coptic, and no shortage of scholars. Horapollo's symbolic tradition held, and Kircher worked within it and got nowhere. What was missing was the idea that the signs might spell sounds.

**An argument from implausibility.** [Cuneiform](cuneiform.md) was resisted because polyvalence looked unusable: if one sign could be several syllables, the Assyrians themselves could not have read it, so the decipherment had to be false. That is an argument about what a writing system could plausibly be, and it was answered by experiment in 1857.

**Authority and politics.** [Mayan hieroglyphs](maya-script.md) were held up by J. Eric S. Thompson's standing and by Cold War suspicion of Knorozov's Soviet citizenship. This is the only obstacle in the whole vault with no epistemic content whatever.

**Physical inaccessibility.** [Behistun](../places/behistun.md) is the odd one. A long trilingual survived intact and was simply out of reach until Rawlinson was willing to hang off a cliff for hours at a time. The corpus was not lost, small, or unreadable. It was up a mountain.

To which the vault should add a fifth, visible across two of the cases:

**An unrecognized key.** Landa's alphabet was in print from the sixteenth century and was treated as a muddle until Knorozov read it as a list of syllables. Medieval Arab scholars had already thought to compare hieroglyphs with Coptic and got no further. In both cases the document that would unlock the script was available and nobody knew what they were holding. A key you cannot recognize is not a key.

## What sets the speed once someone is unstuck

Distinct from what blocks a script is what governs how fast it falls afterwards. Phase 2 separated four variables that the vault had been folding into "corpus size".

**Sign inventory.** [Ugaritic](ugaritic-script.md) has thirty signs and was read in about a year, the fastest in the record. Thirty signs tells you the system is alphabetic before you know anything else, and a small consonantal alphabet over a known language family is formally close to a substitution cipher. Set that against cuneiform's six hundred signs and a century of work.

**Access to the language.** The vault now has five configurations, in rough order of how much they help: a correctly guessed relative available from the outset (Ugaritic), living descendants with millions of speakers ([Classic Maya](../Human-Languages/classic-maya.md)), a readable late form of the same language ([Egyptian](../Human-Languages/egyptian.md)), well-studied relatives in the same family ([Akkadian](../Human-Languages/akkadian.md)), and bilingual sign lists left by ancient scribes for a language with no relatives at all ([Sumerian](../Human-Languages/sumerian.md)). Every solved script here had one of the five. No unsolved script here has any.

**Text length as against corpus volume.** These come apart. Only 21 or 22 Linear Elamite inscriptions are known, and most are longer than the longest of the four to five thousand Indus texts. A very small corpus of long texts can be more tractable than a large corpus of short ones.

**Arrangement.** [Proskouriakoff's finding](../references/proskouriakoff-1960.md) depended on consecutive monuments at [Piedras Negras](../places/piedras-negras.md) standing in series. A single stela shows nothing; the evidence was in the ordering. Quantity would not have substituted for it.

## The diagnostic question, and why it is hard from the inside

For any unread script the useful question is now: **is this below the evidentiary threshold, or above it and stuck for some other reason?** The two call for completely different responses. The first says stop, or go and excavate. The second says look for what is being assumed.

The awkwardness is that the second family is only obvious in retrospect. Before 1822, Egyptian looked exactly like a hopeless corpus problem to people who had an abundant corpus in front of them. The wrong theory was invisible because it was the frame everyone shared.

**But the asymmetry is not total, and this is the part worth keeping.** The evidential bound is the only one that can be estimated in advance, because it is a property of the corpus rather than of the people looking at it. You can count signs. You cannot count your own assumptions. So a claim that a script is *undecipherable* is defensible only in family one, and only ever provisionally, since excavation can move the bound; whereas a claim that a script is *merely undeciphered* is unfalsifiable in the pleasant way, since some future frame might always be the missing one.

## The seven unread scripts, diagnosed

Applying the taxonomy to the vault's own unread scripts. **This is reasoning from the figures already recorded on each note, not a new finding from the literature**, and the point of doing it is that a classification which cannot be applied is not worth keeping.

| Script | Family | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Phaistos Disc](phaistos-disc.md) | **One**, cleanly | 241 tokens, 45 types, one object. No segmentation problem, since the signs were stamped. Quantity is the only obstacle there is. |
| [Linear A](linear-a.md) | **One**, plus a removable layer | 1,427 documents averaging about five signs. Below the bound. But the corpus of record is print-only, so the *analyzable* corpus is smaller than the surviving one, which is family two and fixable. |
| [Cypro-Minoan](cypro-minoan.md) | **One**, but conditionally | 243 short inscriptions is marginal; split three ways by the conventional classification it is hopeless. A family-two decision determines the family-one status. |
| [Indus](indus-script.md) | **Undiagnosable** | Cannot be classified until a prior question is settled: whether it encodes language at all. If it does, 13,372 occurrences at 4.6 signs per text puts it in family one on length. |
| [Rongorongo](rongorongo.md) | **Two** | About 14,000 signs, more than the Indus corpus and twice Linear A, and a probable known language in Old Rapanui. Volume is not the obstacle. |
| [Proto-Elamite](proto-elamite.md) | **Two** | About 1,700 tablets is a large corpus. Kelley's own framing is that scholarship has been marginal relative to the script's importance. |
| [Voynich manuscript](voynich-manuscript.md) | **Breaks the categories** | 102 folios of continuous writing, so it cannot be short of text. See below. |

**Only one of the seven is cleanly explained by the vault's central thesis.** That is the result, and it is not what phase 1 implied. Corpus size does real work on Linear A and the Phaistos Disc and rather little elsewhere.

### Three things the diagnosis breaks

**Family one is not about quantity.** It is about whether the evidence determines an answer, and too little text is only one way to fail that. The [Voynich manuscript](voynich-manuscript.md) has abundant text and an **unbounded hypothesis space**: the live options include natural language in an unknown script, an enciphered natural language, a constructed language, and a hoax carrying no information. Until that set is narrowed, more text does not help, because the candidates are not commensurable. This is exactly the qualification [the evidentiary threshold](evidentiary-threshold-and-unicity-distance.md) already makes, that unicity distance needs a defined key space and decipherment has none, and the Voynich is where it bites hardest. That qualification is now developed in [is it writing at all?](is-it-writing-at-all.md), which argues that well-posedness and corpus size are two terms of one expression rather than two problems.

**Under family one, evidence lost and evidence never created are different.** The vault has been assuming corpora shrank. If [Donnelly](../references/donnelly-2025.md) is right that Cypro-Minoan writers worked on mercantile objects outside scribal schools, that corpus was never large, and its evidentiary limit was set at the moment of inscription rather than by three thousand years of attrition. Nothing can be excavated to fix that, which matters, because "go and dig" is the standard answer to family one.

**Family two needs at least two more members**, both from this diagnosis:

- **Insufficient attention.** Proto-Elamite has a large corpus, no dispute about its status, and nobody blocking it. There have simply not been enough people. That is a different failure from a wrong theory or an authority, and unusually, it is the one most easily fixed.

  This one has since been confirmed from the inside. [Englund](../references/englund-2004.md), the scholar most associated with the corpus, writes that the texts "have played an historically minor role relative to early cuneiform" and that their publication "appears to have proceeded with little understanding of the text corpus and the accounting system it represented, and with little attention paid to an accurate representation in hand copies of the texts themselves". He also puts the corpus itself well above Linear A and early Harappan in size. The diagnosis was made here from the vault's own figures and then found already stated by the authority.
- **An interpretive tradition destroyed rather than faded.** Rongorongo's obstacle is not the corpus. It is that the people who could have explained it were killed or died in the colonization of [Rapa Nui](../places/rapa-nui.md), within the documentary record and while observers were present. Every other script here that recovered had *something* from the inside: Landa took down an informant's account for Maya, Coptic preserved Egyptian, Akkadian scribes left Sumerian vocabularies. Rongorongo has none, because the chain broke abruptly enough that nobody wrote one.

That last point deserves stating plainly. Most family-two obstacles were removable, and were removed: theories are replaced, authorities die, cliffs get climbed. **The rongorongo case is the one where the obstacle appears to be permanent**, because the missing thing is testimony that no longer can be given.

## A caution the vault has earned twice

Arguments from implausibility have a bad record here, and the vault entertains one.

[Farmer, Sproat and Witzel](../references/farmer-sproat-witzel-2004.md) argue partly that a literate civilization would have left long texts somewhere, and that the Indus corpus's failure to contain any is evidence it is not writing. That is the same *shape* as the objection to cuneiform in the 1850s, which was wrong, and it is not thereby refuted, because a bad precedent is not a counterargument.

What the precedent does establish is the standard of proof. The cuneiform objection was not defeated by counter-assertion; it was defeated by four people translating the same text in separate rooms and agreeing. The vault should ask the same of the Indus dispute and note that nothing of that kind is available, since there is nothing to translate.

## Open questions

- **This taxonomy is the vault's own and is not drawn from any source.** Every case in it is sourced, but the classification into two families and five mechanisms is a synthesis made here across eight batches. No literature has been consulted on whether anyone has proposed a better one, and someone almost certainly has.
- **The five configurations of language access are ordered by how much they help, and that ordering is asserted rather than argued.** Ugaritic was fastest and had the first, which is one data point.
- **Family two is a list, not a theory.** Wrong theory, implausibility, authority, inaccessibility and unrecognized key are five things that happened, not a principled partition, and they overlap: Thompson's authority defended a wrong theory, and Landa's alphabet was unrecognized partly because of one.
- **The refinements above are not carried back into the seven script notes.** Each still describes its own obstacle in its own terms, and none says which family it is in. That is deliberate for now, since the taxonomy has changed twice while being applied and may change again, but it should be reconciled at the audit.
- **The two new family-two members are each supported by one case.** Insufficient attention rests on Proto-Elamite and destroyed testimony on rongorongo. One instance is not a category.
- **Whether the Indus dispute is itself a family-two obstacle is not settled here.** A field that cannot agree whether its object is writing is stuck in a way that looks structural, but calling that an obstacle presumes the answer.
