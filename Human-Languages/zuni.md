# Zuni

Zuni, *Shiwi'ma* to its speakers, is a living language of the American Southwest, spoken at Zuni Pueblo in western New Mexico. It has no demonstrated relative.

## Classification

Glottolog classifies Zuni as an isolate: a single language, no children, no parent, glottocode `zuni1245` and ISO 639-3 `zun`. The reference given for family membership is Campbell 1997. WALS holds it under the code `zun`, in a family and genus of its own.

Glottolog assesses the language as shifting, citing Campbell and colleagues 2022, whose rating is "Threatened (60 percent certain, based on the evidence available)".

Its isolate status is of a different kind from [Basque](basque.md)'s. Zuni sits among the well-described families of North America, so the absence of a relative is a result reached against dense comparative coverage rather than a gap in the record.

## Attestation

**Not covered here, for want of a source read.** Zuni has no pre-contact written record. Its description is a twentieth-century tradition, principally Bunzel in the 1930s and Newman 1965, and none of it was read for this note.

## Structure

The sketch below is drawn from WALS, which codes each value against named grammars; those grammars are named here because the claim is theirs. Coverage is reasonable at 89 datapoints.

The sound system is unremarkable on every measure WALS applies: average consonant inventory, average vowel quality inventory at five or six, average consonant-to-vowel ratio, and no uncommon consonants, on Newman 1965 and Walker 1972. Against [Hadza](hadza.md) and [Sandawe](sandawe.md) that is the contrast worth noting, since an isolate need not be phonologically unusual and these three show both outcomes.

Zuni is verb-final, with SOV order, postpositions, genitive before noun and adjective after noun, coded on Cook 1975, Newman 1965 and Nichols 1997b and 1999. There is no grammatical gender, on Newman 1965. Alignment of verbal person marking is neutral, on Bunzel 1933-1938.

**WALS carries two codings of case for Zuni that do not agree.** The feature on the number of cases records no morphological case-marking, on Newman 1965; the feature on the position of case affixes records case suffixes, on Nichols 1997b. Both are in the atlas against the same language, from different chapters resting on different sources, and no attempt is made here to decide between them. The same conflict appears for [Nivkh](nivkh.md), which suggests it is a property of how the atlas is assembled rather than a peculiarity of either language.

The distinctive value is negation. WALS codes Zuni as having obligatory double negation, on Nichols 1997b, where the other isolates covered here use a single negative particle or affix.

## Open questions

- **No speaker figure is recorded here.** Ethnologue holds the standard count behind a paywall.
- **The two case codings conflict**, as noted above. Settling it needs Newman 1965 read directly, since the disagreement is with a later chapter author rather than within one description.
- **Campbell 1997 has not been read.** It is the reference Glottolog cites for the classification, and it is also the standard survey of American Indian languages, so it would serve several notes at once.
- **Nothing here covers proposed external relationships.** Zuni has been linked to Penutian and to other groupings; naming the proposals and their critics needs sources not read here.
