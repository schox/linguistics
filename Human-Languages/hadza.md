# Hadza

Hadza is a living language of northern Tanzania, spoken around Lake Eyasi. It is one of two click languages of East Africa with no demonstrated relative, the other being [Sandawe](sandawe.md), and it is the better known of the two for having been grouped with them.

## Classification

Glottolog classifies Hadza as an isolate: a single language, no children, no parent, glottocode `hadz1240` and ISO 639-3 `hts`. The reference given for family membership is Güldemann 2018, at pages 98 to 107. WALS holds it under the code `had`, in a family and genus of its own.

Hadza was long filed under "Khoisan", and that label is areal rather than genealogical. What the languages so grouped share is click phonology, which is a typological feature and not evidence of common descent, and the classifications used here do not treat Khoisan as a family. The point is made in this area's index and it applies to Hadza and Sandawe alike: two languages with clicks and no relatives are two isolates, not a family of two.

Glottolog assesses the language as shifting, citing Campbell and colleagues 2022, whose rating is "Threatened (40 percent certain, based on the evidence available)".

## Structure

**WALS coverage of Hadza is thin, and the sketch below is correspondingly thin.** The atlas holds 13 datapoints for this language against 149 for [Basque](basque.md), and they are concentrated in phonology. Nothing in WALS as read here records its word order, its case system or its alignment, so this note does not state them. That is a fact about how well the language is described in the instrument, not about the language.

What WALS does record, on Sands and colleagues 1996 and Tucker and colleagues 1977: a large consonant inventory, a high consonant-to-vowel ratio, and an average vowel quality inventory of five or six. The consonant inventory is large because of the clicks, and WALS codes their presence explicitly under the feature for uncommon consonants.
