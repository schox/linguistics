# Ugaritic

An extinct Northwest Semitic language of the Late Bronze Age Levant, recorded in the [Ugaritic script](../Decipherment/ugaritic-script.md) at [Ugarit](../places/ugarit.md).

## The language was guessed before it was known

This is the case where identifying the family came first and made everything else fast.

The excavators supposed the language was closely related to Hebrew and Phoenician, on no evidence beyond context and the shape of the problem, and they were right. Because Northwest Semitic was thoroughly studied, a correct guess about the family delivered a working phonological and lexical model immediately, and a thirty-letter alphabet plus a known plaintext model is a tractable problem.

Compare the vault's other configurations:

- [Egyptian](egyptian.md): a late form of the same language survived and was readable.
- [Akkadian](akkadian.md): no descendant, but well-studied relatives in the same family.
- [Sumerian](sumerian.md): an isolate, read because bilingual sign lists survived.
- [Classic Maya](classic-maya.md): living descendants with millions of speakers.
- **Ugaritic: relatives known, and the relationship correctly assumed at the outset.**

The last is the most favourable starting position of the five, and it produced the fastest decipherment. That is not a coincidence, and it is the vault's clearest evidence that access to the language, rather than the size of the corpus, is what decides how quickly a script falls once someone has the right idea about the signs.

## Why it mattered beyond decipherment

Ugaritic gave scholars a substantial body of Northwest Semitic literature roughly contemporary with, and independent of, the biblical texts. The CREWS Project's judgment is that the tablets "revolutionised our understanding of the Late Bronze Age Levant and of the early history of the alphabet".

That second clause is the part this vault should follow up, and has not.

## Open questions

- **This is a stub**, written so the script has a language to point at, as with the other four. It has no grammar, no account of the corpus, and nothing on the literature.
- **The claim that Northwest Semitic scholarship supplied a ready model is reasoning, not citation.** It follows from what the sources say about the guess being correct and the decipherment being fast, but no source read here states the mechanism, and under the vault's rule on derived claims that should be marked as it is here.
- **The relationship to Phoenician and Hebrew is asserted at the level the sources give it** and not worked out. Whether Ugaritic is a Canaanite language or a separate Northwest Semitic branch is a real question in the literature and is not addressed.
