# Tyrsenian

Tyrsenian is a proposed language family grouping Etruscan with Raetic and Lemnian. It is a hypothesis about relatedness, not an accepted classification, and the two authorities used in this area disagree about it.

## The proposal

The grouping is associated with Helmut Rix, in work of 1998 and 2008. Its appeal is straightforward: three fragmentary languages of the pre-Roman and early Roman Mediterranean, none with an accepted relative outside the set, showing resemblances to one another. If the grouping holds, [Etruscan](etruscan.md) is not an isolate but a member of a small family, and a decipherer of any one of the three has two fragmentary relatives to compare against rather than none.

## What Glottolog does with it

**Glottolog has no Tyrsenian languoid.** A search of Glottolog 5.3 returns no match for the name, and Etruscan (`etru1241`), Raetic (`raet1238`) and Lemnian (`lemn1237`) are each top-level languoids with no parent: three isolates, not one family.

The reason is given on the Etruscan record, which cites Simona Marchesini 2009 for family membership and carries her position in these words: "Contra Helmut Rix 2008, Helmut Rix 1998 it is advisable to take a more cautious position with respect to the genealogical relatedness of Rhaetian with Etruscan".

So the disagreement is attributable on both sides. Rix argues the relationship; Marchesini counsels caution; Glottolog, which this area adopts as its classification, follows Marchesini and files the three separately.

## What follows for the notes here

[Etruscan](etruscan.md) states that the current mainstream view places it in Tyrsenian, and that sentence is now qualified in that note. The claim is defensible in the Etruscological literature and it is not what the classification this area adopts records, which is a difference worth showing rather than resolving by preference.

The `family` field on Etruscan's note reads `Tyrsenian`, and this area's index lists Tyrsenian as a small family in its section on isolates. Both were written before the classification was checked. Whether to change them is a decision about which authority the area follows when its sources disagree, and it is left open here rather than settled in passing.

## Open questions

- **Neither Rix 1998 nor Rix 2008 has been read**, and neither has Marchesini 2009. All three are named here on Glottolog's citation of them, which is enough to attribute the dispute and not enough to characterize the arguments.
- **The evidence for the grouping is not described here**, because no source stating it was read. What resemblances between Etruscan, Raetic and Lemnian are claimed, and how many, is exactly what would let a reader judge.
- **Whether Lemnian is better treated as a variety of Etruscan than as a sister** is a question the sources read do not address, and it changes what the grouping amounts to.
- **How the vault should reconcile a `family` field with a classification that rejects it** is unresolved, as set out above.
