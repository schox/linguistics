# Ainu

Ainu is the language of the Ainu people of northern Japan, with historical range across Hokkaido, Sakhalin and the Kuril Islands. Glottolog assesses it as nearly extinct, classifying it as critically endangered with 80 percent certainty.

## Classification

Ainu has no demonstrated relative, and Glottolog treats it not as a single isolate language but as a small family. The family languoid is `ainu1252`, and beneath it sit three languages: Hokkaido Ainu (`ainu1240`, ISO 639-3 `ain`, with Hokkaido and Taraika as dialects), Kuril Ainu (`kuri1271`) and Sakhalin Ainu (`sakh1245`). Glottolog cites Janhunen 2021 for the subclassification.

The identifiers on this note therefore point at Hokkaido Ainu rather than at the family, because that is the languoid the ISO code attaches to and the one WALS describes. A note that treated "Ainu" as a single language would be flattening a distinction its own sources make, and the flattening is recorded here rather than performed silently.

WALS includes Ainu in its 200-language sample but not in the 100-language sample, under the code `ain`.

## Attestation

**Not covered here, for want of a source read.** Ainu is a language with a large recorded oral literature and no indigenous writing system, and the documentary record is overwhelmingly the product of outside transcription. Characterizing that record properly, including the *yukar* epic tradition and the history of its collection, needs a source this note does not have. The gap is stated rather than filled: see the open questions.

## Structure

The sketch below is drawn from WALS, which codes each value against named grammars; those grammars are named here because the claim is theirs.

Ainu is verb-final, with SOV order, postpositions, genitive before noun and adjective before noun, coded on Shibatani 1990, Refsing 1986 and Dettmer 1989.

Its sound system is small: WALS codes the consonant inventory as small, the vowel quality inventory as average at five or six, and the consonant-to-vowel ratio as moderately low, on Patrie 1982 and Simeon 1969. Among the four isolates written up together here it is the only one with a small consonant inventory.

The morphology is what distinguishes it. WALS codes inflection as equally prefixing and suffixing, on Dettmer 1989, Refsing 1986 and Shibatani 1990, and records no grammatical gender. Case is the unusual value: WALS codes Ainu as having exclusively borderline case-marking, and separately records no case affixes or adpositional clitics, on Tamura 2000, Refsing 1986, Shibatani 1990 and Dettmer 1989. That is a coding decision about what counts as a case, and it means the language does not fit the inventory-of-cases question the feature asks.

Alignment of case marking of full noun phrases is coded neutral, on Refsing 1986, while alignment of verbal person marking is coded split, on Shibatani 1990. Person is marked on the verb rather than carried by independent pronouns.

## Open questions

- **No speaker figure is recorded here**, and for a critically endangered language the figure is both the most asked-for fact and the one that moves fastest. Ethnologue holds the standard count behind a paywall and was not reached for this note.
- **Attestation is not covered at all.** Bugaeva's *Handbook of the Ainu Language* (De Gruyter, 2022) is the obvious modern reference and is not open access; a copy circulating on the Internet Archive was found and deliberately not used, since its licensing is not clear.
- **Janhunen 2021 has not been read.** It is the reference Glottolog cites for the three-way subclassification, and that structure is stated here on Glottolog's authority rather than on Janhunen's.
- **Proposed external relationships are not covered**, including the comparisons to Japonic and the older Altaic proposals. Attributing them needs their proponents and critics, and none was read.
