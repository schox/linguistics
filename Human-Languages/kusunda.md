# Kusunda

Kusunda is a language of Nepal with no demonstrated relative, and the most severely endangered of the isolates covered here. Glottolog records it as nearly extinct.

## Classification

Glottolog classifies Kusunda as an isolate: a single language, no children, no parent, glottocode `kusu1250` and ISO 639-3 `kgg`. The reference given for family membership is Gerber 2017. WALS holds it under the code `kus`, in a family and genus of its own.

The endangerment assessment is the most definite of the four in this batch, and the certainty is the point. Glottolog cites Campbell and colleagues 2022, rating Kusunda "Critically Endangered (100 percent certain, based on the evidence available)", with the supporting observation, quoted there from Gautam 2012, that other Kusunda people "can only speak a few Kusunda words, but can't communicate [fully] in the language". A hundred percent certainty is unusual in that dataset and reflects how little is in dispute.

## Structure

**The sketch below is thin, and its thinness is the finding.** WALS holds 17 datapoints for Kusunda, against 89 for [Zuni](zuni.md) and 149 for [Basque](basque.md), and every value read here rests on a single source, Reinhard and Toba 1970. A description of a language now down to a handful of speakers, resting on one sketch from 1970, is a different evidential situation from the others in this subfield, and the note states it rather than presenting five features as a characterization.

What WALS records, all on Reinhard and Toba 1970: SOV order, postpositions, tense-aspect suffixes, and negation by affix following the verb.

Nothing in WALS as read here records its phonology, its case system, its alignment or its gender, so this note does not state them.

## Open questions

- **No speaker figure is recorded here**, and for Kusunda this is the fact most readers would want. Ethnologue's count is behind a paywall, and published counts of last speakers are exactly the figures that go stale fastest.
- **Gerber 2017 has not been read.** It is the reference Glottolog cites for the classification.
- **Reinhard and Toba 1970 has not been read either**, and everything in the structure section rests on it at one remove through WALS. For a language this poorly attested, the difference between reading the sketch and reading the atlas is larger than usual.
- **Recent documentation is not covered.** Fieldwork on Kusunda has continued and is reported to have recovered material well beyond the 1970 sketch, but no such work was located and read for this note, so nothing is claimed about what it found.
- **Proposed external relationships are not covered**, including comparisons that would place Kusunda with other languages of the region. Naming them needs their proponents and critics.
