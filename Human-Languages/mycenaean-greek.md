# Mycenaean Greek

The earliest attested form of the Greek language, recorded in [Linear B](../Decipherment/linear-b.md) on the Greek mainland and Crete from about 1450 to 1200 BCE.

## Why it matters here

Mycenaean Greek is the reason Linear B was solvable. Ventris's 1952 result was that the script recorded Greek, and once that was granted the whole apparatus of Greek historical linguistics became available to check and extend the reading. The decipherment succeeded because the underlying language turned out to be known, which is the *different script, same language* case, the tractable one.

Set against Etruscan, the pair makes the vault's central argument concrete. Both are ancient languages of the Mediterranean; both are written in scripts we can read. Mycenaean Greek is understood because it has a vast attested descendant literature. [Etruscan](etruscan.md) is not, because it has almost nothing. The difference is comparative leverage, not scribal difficulty.
