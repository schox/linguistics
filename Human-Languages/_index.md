# Human Languages

The particular languages, organized genealogically. Genealogy is the canonical spine because it is what makes the comparative and decipherment material navigable: knowing a language's relatives is precisely what lets you read it, or fail to.

A dagger marks an extinct language. Classification below follows mainstream consensus; contested groupings are flagged as such, because in this area the disputes matter and a taxonomy that hides them is misleading.

## Two separate vocabularies

This index carries **two** controlled vocabularies, and they are not the same list.

**`family`** takes a top-level family name: the bold heading of a numbered section below, or a bolded family within it where that family is top-level in its own right. Examples: `Indo-European`, `Afro-Asiatic`, `Sino-Tibetan`, `Uralic`, `Turkic`, `Dravidian`, `Austronesian`, `Atlantic-Congo`, `Pama-Nyungan`, `Tyrsenian`, `Kartvelian`. Use `branch` for the level below (`Germanic`, `Semitic`, `Hellenic`, `Iranian`), and leave `branch` empty for an isolate or a single-member family.

**`subfield`** takes one of the ten section headings listed here, which is a coarser cut used for filtering across the area:

1. Indo-European
2. Afro-Asiatic
3. Sino-Tibetan
4. Other Eurasian families
5. Austronesian and Papuan
6. African families
7. Languages of the Americas
8. Australian languages
9. Isolates and small families
10. Non-genealogical groupings

A language may carry more than one `subfield`, most usefully adding **Ancient corpus languages** from section 10 where it is a dead language known only from a fixed corpus, since that is the property the Decipherment area cares about.

## Sections

### 1. Indo-European

- **[Germanic](germanic.md)**: North (Icelandic, Faroese, Norwegian, Swedish, Danish), West ([English](english.md), Frisian, Dutch, Afrikaans, [German](german.md), Yiddish), East (Gothic †)
- **[Italic and Romance](italic-and-romance.md)**: [Latin](latin.md), [French](french.md), [Spanish](spanish.md), [Portuguese](portuguese.md), Galician, Catalan, Occitan, Italian, Sardinian, Romansh, Romanian; Oscan † and Umbrian †
- **[Hellenic](hellenic.md)**: [Mycenaean](mycenaean-greek.md) †, [Ancient Greek](ancient-greek.md) dialects, Koine, Modern Greek, Tsakonian
- **[Celtic](celtic.md)**: Goidelic (Irish, Scottish Gaelic, Manx), Brythonic (Welsh, Breton, Cornish), Continental (Gaulish †, Celtiberian †)
- **[Balto-Slavic](balto-slavic.md)**: Baltic (Lithuanian, Latvian, Old Prussian †); Slavic East ([Russian](russian.md), Ukrainian, Belarusian), West (Polish, Czech, Slovak, Sorbian), South (Serbo-Croatian, Slovene, Bulgarian, Macedonian, Old Church Slavonic †)
- **[Indo-Iranian](indo-iranian.md)**: Indo-Aryan ([Sanskrit](sanskrit.md) †, Pali †, Prakrits †, [Hindi](hindi.md)-Urdu, [Bengali](bengali.md), Punjabi, Marathi, Gujarati, Sinhala, Romani); Iranian ([Old Persian](old-persian.md) †, Avestan †, Middle Persian †, [Persian](persian.md), Kurdish, Pashto, Ossetian, Sogdian †)
- **[Armenian](armenian.md)**, **[Albanian](albanian.md)**
- **[Anatolian](anatolian.md) †**: [Hittite](hittite.md), Luvian, Palaic, Lydian, Lycian, Carian. Junction with Decipherment.
- **[Tocharian](tocharian.md) †**: A and B
- **[Proto-Indo-European](proto-indo-european.md)**: reconstruction, the laryngeal theory, homeland hypotheses, the ancient DNA evidence

### 2. Afro-Asiatic

- **[Semitic](semitic.md)**: East ([Akkadian](akkadian.md) †, Eblaite †); Northwest ([Ugaritic](ugaritic.md) †, Phoenician and Punic †, Amorite †, [Hebrew](hebrew.md), Aramaic and Syriac); Central and South (Arabic and its varieties, of which [Modern Standard Arabic](modern-standard-arabic.md) is the written register, Old South Arabian †, Ge'ez, [Amharic](amharic.md), Tigrinya, Mehri)
- **[Egyptian](egyptian.md)**: Old, Middle and Late Egyptian †, Demotic †, [Coptic](coptic.md)
- **Berber (Amazigh)**: Tashelhit, Kabyle, Tuareg (Tamasheq)
- **[Cushitic](cushitic.md)**: [Somali](somali.md), Oromo, Afar, Beja
- **[Chadic](chadic.md)**: [Hausa](hausa.md)
- **[Omotic](omotic.md)**: membership contested

### 3. Sino-Tibetan

- **[Sinitic](sinitic.md)**: Old Chinese †, Middle Chinese †, [Mandarin](mandarin-chinese.md), Yue ([Cantonese](cantonese.md)), Wu, Min, Hakka, Xiang, Gan
- **[Tibeto-Burman](tibeto-burman.md)**: Tibetic, [Burmese](burmese.md), Karen, Bodo-Garo, Naga; Tangut †

### 4. Other Eurasian families

- **[Uralic](uralic.md)**: Finnic ([Finnish](finnish.md), Estonian), Sámi, [Hungarian](hungarian.md), Mordvinic, Permic, Samoyedic
- **[Turkic](turkic.md)**: [Turkish](turkish.md), Azerbaijani, Uzbek, Kazakh, Kyrgyz, Uyghur, Chuvash; Old Turkic † (Orkhon inscriptions)
- **[Mongolic](mongolic.md)** ([Mongolian](mongolian.md)) and **[Tungusic](tungusic.md)**; the **Altaic** hypothesis, now generally rejected as a genealogical grouping
- **[Japonic](japonic.md)**: [Japanese](japanese.md), Old Japanese †, Ryukyuan
- **[Koreanic](koreanic.md)**: [Korean](korean.md), Middle Korean †, Jeju
- **[Kra-Dai](kra-dai.md)**: [Thai](thai.md), Lao, Zhuang
- **[Austroasiatic](austroasiatic.md)**: [Vietnamese](vietnamese.md), Khmer, Munda
- **[Dravidian](dravidian.md)**: [Tamil](tamil.md), Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Brahui. Junction with Decipherment via the Indus substrate hypothesis.
- **Caucasian**: [Kartvelian](kartvelian.md) ([Georgian](georgian.md)), [Northeast Caucasian](northeast-caucasian.md) (Chechen, Avar), [Northwest Caucasian](northwest-caucasian.md) (Abkhaz, Circassian, Ubykh †). Three separate families, not one.
- **[Hurro-Urartian](hurro-urartian.md) †**

### 5. Austronesian and Papuan

- **Austronesian**: [Formosan languages](formosan-languages.md); [Malayo-Polynesian](malayo-polynesian.md) (Malay and [Indonesian](indonesian.md), Javanese, [Tagalog](tagalog.md), Cebuano, [Malagasy](malagasy.md)); [Oceanic](oceanic.md) (Fijian, Samoan, Tongan, [Māori](maori.md), [Hawaiian](hawaiian.md), [Rapanui](rapanui.md)). [The Out-of-Taiwan hypothesis](out-of-taiwan-hypothesis.md).
- **Trans-New Guinea** and the wider Papuan situation, which is an areal problem rather than a single family

### 6. African families

- **[Atlantic-Congo](atlantic-congo.md) / Niger-Congo**: [Bantu](bantu.md) ([Swahili](swahili.md), [Zulu](zulu.md), Xhosa, Shona, Kikuyu), [Yoruba](yoruba.md), [Igbo](igbo.md), Akan, Fula, Wolof
- **[Nilo-Saharan](nilo-saharan.md)**: Nilotic (Dinka, Luo, Maasai), Songhay. Grouping contested.
- **Khoe-Kwadi, Tuu, Kxa**: the languages formerly lumped as "Khoisan", which is an areal label (click [phonology](../General-Linguistics/phonology.md)) and not a family. Includes Khoekhoe, Taa, [Hadza](hadza.md) and [Sandawe](sandawe.md), the last two probably isolates.

### 7. Languages of the Americas

- **[Na-Dene](na-dene.md)**: [Navajo](navajo.md), Apache, Tlingit
- **[Algic](algic.md)**: Cree, Ojibwe, Blackfoot
- **[Iroquoian](iroquoian.md)**: Cherokee, Mohawk
- **[Siouan](siouan.md)**, **[Muskogean](muskogean.md)**, **[Salishan](salishan.md)**
- **[Uto-Aztecan](uto-aztecan.md)**: [Nahuatl](nahuatl.md), Hopi, O'odham
- **[Mayan](mayan.md)**: [Classic Maya](classic-maya.md) †, [Yucatec](yucatec-maya.md), K'iche', Ch'ol. Junction with Decipherment.
- **[Oto-Manguean](oto-manguean.md)**: Zapotec, Mixtec
- **[Quechuan](quechuan.md)** ([Quechua](quechua.md)), **[Aymaran](aymaran.md)**, **[Arawakan](arawakan.md)**, **[Tupian](tupian.md)** ([Guaraní](guarani.md)), **[Cariban](cariban.md)**

### 8. Australian languages

- **[Pama-Nyungan](pama-nyungan.md)**: [Noongar](noongar.md), Wiradjuri, Kaurna, [Warlpiri](warlpiri.md), Arrernte, Western Desert (Pitjantjatjara, Ngaanyatjarra), Yolngu Matha, Guugu Yimithirr
- **[Non-Pama-Nyungan](non-pama-nyungan.md)**: Gunwinyguan, Bunuban, Nyulnyulan, Worrorran, Iwaidjan, Tiwi
- **Contact varieties**: Kriol, Torres Strait Creole, Aboriginal English
- **Documentation and revival**: the Noongar and Kaurna revival programs, archival sources, the ethics of working with community-held material
- **Structural interest**: kinship systems, avoidance registers, absolute spatial reference, initiation languages

### 9. Isolates and small families

Bolded here like every other section, so that each yields a usable `family` value.

- **[Basque](basque.md)** (Euskara), the only surviving pre-Indo-European language of western Europe
- **[Sumerian](sumerian.md) †**, **[Elamite](elamite.md) †**, **Hurro-Urartian †**
- **[Tyrsenian](tyrsenian.md) †**: [Etruscan](etruscan.md), Raetic, Lemnian. Note that Etruscan is frequently described as an isolate in older literature; the current mainstream view makes it Tyrsenian, a small family.
- **[Ainu](ainu.md)**, **[Burushaski](burushaski.md)**, **[Nivkh](nivkh.md)**, **[Kusunda](kusunda.md)**, **[Zuni](zuni.md)**
- **Hadza**, **Sandawe**, formerly lumped under "Khoisan"

These are junctions with Decipherment. An isolate offers no comparative leverage at all, and a small family of fragmentary relatives offers very little, which is precisely what makes a script recording one hard to read. See [Etruscan](etruscan.md) for the worked case.

### 10. Non-genealogical groupings

- **Sign languages**: [Auslan](auslan.md), ASL, BSL, LSF, [village sign languages](village-sign-languages.md) (Kata Kolok, Al-Sayyid Bedouin), [Nicaraguan Sign Language](nicaraguan-sign-language.md) and the emergence question. Full languages with their own phonology and [syntax](../General-Linguistics/syntax.md).
- **Pidgins and creoles**: [Tok Pisin](tok-pisin.md), Bislama, [Haitian Creole](haitian-creole.md), Kriol, the substrate and bioprogram debates
- **Constructed languages**: auxiliary ([Esperanto](esperanto.md), Interlingua, Volapük), engineered (Lojban, Ithkuil), artistic (Quenya and Sindarin, Klingon, Dothraki). Junction with Decipherment via the Voynich hypotheses.
- **Ancient corpus languages**: the practical question of what is actually readable, and how much text survives for each. This is where corpus size, and therefore decipherability, is recorded.

## What is stubbed, and what is not

**The genealogical spine is stubbed**, 88 notes added 2026-08-12: the families and branches named in the sections above, plus the topics of the four non-genealogical groupings. Family and branch stubs cite [Glottolog](../references/glottolog.md) as the classification this area adopts, and each records that its `glottocode` is still owed.

**51 individual languages are stubbed**, added 2026-08-13 on Andrew's criterion of the most spoken languages plus one or two from each family, which is the criterion already applied to Computer Languages on 2026-08-12. The sections above name roughly 199 languages; stubbing all of them would be the "every language that ever existed" outcome this area is meant to avoid, and 51 across 24 families is a spread rather than a survey.

**The popularity half rests on four languages and no more.** [Ethnologue 2026](../references/ethnologue-2026.md) is the standard ranking and its ranked list is sold rather than published: the free pages give the top four in prose and nothing further. So Mandarin Chinese, Spanish, English and Hindi are here on sourced speaker numbers, and the other 47 are here for genealogical coverage. Every stub says which of the two it is. Reaching the full list, by purchase or through an institution, would let the popularity half be done properly; until then the split is stated rather than blurred.

The coverage half is a judgement and is recorded as one. It was made from this index's own sections, so it inherits their classification and nothing else, and it takes in six extinct languages (Latin, Ancient Greek, Sanskrit, Old Persian, Hittite, Coptic) that no ranking of speakers could reach. The last four were owed anyway, because notes elsewhere already refer to them.

**WALS was passed over as the selection instrument**, having been this area's candidate since the taxonomy was cut. [WALS 2013](../references/wals-2013.md) defines a 100-language sample and a further 100, on the stated ground that "a general desideratum for a good language sample is that it maximize both genealogical and areal diversity", which is a published answer to the question and a better one on its own terms. Two things ruled it out here: the sample optimizes for typological diversity rather than prominence, so it cannot supply the popularity half at all and omits the classical corpus languages Decipherment turns on; and the list was not retrievable from the WALS site in a usable form on 2026-08-12, being served through a JavaScript table rather than as a file. It is still owed as the instrument for the typology work in General Linguistics, which is what it was built for. See `DECISIONS.md`, 2026-08-13.

**Nine isolates were retyped** from `Concept` to `Language` in the same change: Ainu, Basque, Burushaski, Elamite, Hadza, Kusunda, Nivkh, Sandawe and Zuni, all listed under section 9 above. For an isolate the family and the language are the same thing, so typing them as groupings misdescribed them.

See `ROADMAP.md`.
