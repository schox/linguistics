# Human Languages

The particular languages, organized genealogically. Genealogy is the canonical spine because it is what makes the comparative and decipherment material navigable: knowing a language's relatives is precisely what lets you read it, or fail to.

A dagger marks an extinct language. Classification below follows mainstream consensus; contested groupings are flagged as such, because in this area the disputes matter and a taxonomy that hides them is misleading.

## Two separate vocabularies

This index carries **two** controlled vocabularies, and they are not the same list.

**`family`** takes a top-level family name: the bold heading of a numbered section below, or a bolded family within it where that family is top-level in its own right. Examples: `Indo-European`, `Afro-Asiatic`, `Sino-Tibetan`, `Uralic`, `Turkic`, `Dravidian`, `Austronesian`, `Atlantic-Congo`, `Pama-Nyungan`, `Tyrsenian`, `Kartvelian`. Use `branch` for the level below (`Germanic`, `Semitic`, `Hellenic`, `Iranian`), and leave `branch` empty for an isolate or a single-member family.

**`subfield`** takes one of the ten section headings listed here, which is a coarser cut used for filtering across the area:

1. Indo-European
2. Afro-Asiatic
3. Sino-Tibetan
4. Other Eurasian families
5. Austronesian and Papuan
6. African families
7. Languages of the Americas
8. Australian languages
9. Isolates and small families
10. Non-genealogical groupings

A language may carry more than one `subfield`, most usefully adding **Ancient corpus languages** from section 10 where it is a dead language known only from a fixed corpus, since that is the property the Decipherment area cares about.

## Sections

### 1. Indo-European

- **Germanic**: North (Icelandic, Faroese, Norwegian, Swedish, Danish), West (English, Frisian, Dutch, Afrikaans, German, Yiddish), East (Gothic †)
- **Italic and Romance**: Latin, French, Spanish, Portuguese, Galician, Catalan, Occitan, Italian, Sardinian, Romansh, Romanian; Oscan † and Umbrian †
- **Hellenic**: Mycenaean †, Ancient Greek dialects, Koine, Modern Greek, Tsakonian
- **Celtic**: Goidelic (Irish, Scottish Gaelic, Manx), Brythonic (Welsh, Breton, Cornish), Continental (Gaulish †, Celtiberian †)
- **Balto-Slavic**: Baltic (Lithuanian, Latvian, Old Prussian †); Slavic East (Russian, Ukrainian, Belarusian), West (Polish, Czech, Slovak, Sorbian), South (Serbo-Croatian, Slovene, Bulgarian, Macedonian, Old Church Slavonic †)
- **Indo-Iranian**: Indo-Aryan (Sanskrit †, Pali †, Prakrits †, Hindi-Urdu, Bengali, Punjabi, Marathi, Gujarati, Sinhala, Romani); Iranian (Old Persian †, Avestan †, Middle Persian †, Persian, Kurdish, Pashto, Ossetian, Sogdian †)
- **Armenian**, **Albanian**
- **Anatolian †**: Hittite, Luvian, Palaic, Lydian, Lycian, Carian. Junction with Decipherment.
- **Tocharian †**: A and B
- **Proto-Indo-European**: reconstruction, the laryngeal theory, homeland hypotheses, the ancient DNA evidence

### 2. Afro-Asiatic

- **Semitic**: East (Akkadian †, Eblaite †); Northwest (Ugaritic †, Phoenician and Punic †, Amorite †, Hebrew, Aramaic and Syriac); Central and South (Arabic and its varieties, Old South Arabian †, Ge'ez, Amharic, Tigrinya, Mehri)
- **Egyptian**: Old, Middle and Late Egyptian †, Demotic †, Coptic
- **Berber (Amazigh)**: Tashelhit, Kabyle, Tuareg (Tamasheq)
- **Cushitic**: Somali, Oromo, Afar, Beja
- **Chadic**: Hausa
- **Omotic**: membership contested

### 3. Sino-Tibetan

- **Sinitic**: Old Chinese †, Middle Chinese †, Mandarin, Yue (Cantonese), Wu, Min, Hakka, Xiang, Gan
- **Tibeto-Burman**: Tibetic, Burmese, Karen, Bodo-Garo, Naga; Tangut †

### 4. Other Eurasian families

- **Uralic**: Finnic (Finnish, Estonian), Sámi, Hungarian, Mordvinic, Permic, Samoyedic
- **Turkic**: Turkish, Azerbaijani, Uzbek, Kazakh, Kyrgyz, Uyghur, Chuvash; Old Turkic † (Orkhon inscriptions)
- **Mongolic** and **Tungusic**; the **Altaic** hypothesis, now generally rejected as a genealogical grouping
- **Japonic**: Japanese, Old Japanese †, Ryukyuan
- **Koreanic**: Korean, Middle Korean †, Jeju
- **Kra-Dai**: Thai, Lao, Zhuang
- **Austroasiatic**: Vietnamese, Khmer, Munda
- **Dravidian**: Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Brahui. Junction with Decipherment via the Indus substrate hypothesis.
- **Caucasian**: Kartvelian (Georgian), Northeast Caucasian (Chechen, Avar), Northwest Caucasian (Abkhaz, Circassian, Ubykh †). Three separate families, not one.
- **Hurro-Urartian †**

### 5. Austronesian and Papuan

- **Austronesian**: Formosan languages; Malayo-Polynesian (Malay and Indonesian, Javanese, Tagalog, Cebuano, Malagasy); Oceanic (Fijian, Samoan, Tongan, Māori, Hawaiian). The Out-of-Taiwan hypothesis.
- **Trans-New Guinea** and the wider Papuan situation, which is an areal problem rather than a single family

### 6. African families

- **Atlantic-Congo / Niger-Congo**: Bantu (Swahili, Zulu, Xhosa, Shona, Kikuyu), Yoruba, Igbo, Akan, Fula, Wolof
- **Nilo-Saharan**: Nilotic (Dinka, Luo, Maasai), Songhay. Grouping contested.
- **Khoe-Kwadi, Tuu, Kxa**: the languages formerly lumped as "Khoisan", which is an areal label (click phonology) and not a family. Includes Khoekhoe, Taa, Hadza and Sandawe, the last two probably isolates.

### 7. Languages of the Americas

- **Na-Dene**: Navajo, Apache, Tlingit
- **Algic**: Cree, Ojibwe, Blackfoot
- **Iroquoian**: Cherokee, Mohawk
- **Siouan**, **Muskogean**, **Salishan**
- **Uto-Aztecan**: Nahuatl, Hopi, O'odham
- **Mayan**: Classic Maya †, Yucatec, K'iche', Ch'ol. Junction with Decipherment.
- **Oto-Manguean**: Zapotec, Mixtec
- **Quechuan**, **Aymaran**, **Arawakan**, **Tupian** (Guaraní), **Cariban**
- **Inuit-Yupik-Unangan**: Inuktitut, Kalaallisut, Yupik

### 8. Australian languages

- **Pama-Nyungan**: Noongar, Wiradjuri, Kaurna, Warlpiri, Arrernte, Western Desert (Pitjantjatjara, Ngaanyatjarra), Yolngu Matha, Guugu Yimithirr
- **Non-Pama-Nyungan**: Gunwinyguan, Bunuban, Nyulnyulan, Worrorran, Iwaidjan, Tiwi
- **Contact varieties**: Kriol, Torres Strait Creole, Aboriginal English
- **Documentation and revival**: the Noongar and Kaurna revival programs, archival sources, the ethics of working with community-held material
- **Structural interest**: kinship systems, avoidance registers, absolute spatial reference, initiation languages

### 9. Isolates and small families

Bolded here like every other section, so that each yields a usable `family` value.

- **Basque** (Euskara), the only surviving pre-Indo-European language of western Europe
- **Sumerian †**, **Elamite †**, **Hurro-Urartian †**
- **Tyrsenian †**: Etruscan, Raetic, Lemnian. Note that Etruscan is frequently described as an isolate in older literature; the current mainstream view makes it Tyrsenian, a small family.
- **Ainu**, **Burushaski**, **Nivkh**, **Kusunda**, **Zuni**
- **Hadza**, **Sandawe**, formerly lumped under "Khoisan"

These are junctions with Decipherment. An isolate offers no comparative leverage at all, and a small family of fragmentary relatives offers very little, which is precisely what makes a script recording one hard to read. See [Etruscan](etruscan.md) for the worked case.

### 10. Non-genealogical groupings

- **Sign languages**: Auslan, ASL, BSL, LSF, village sign languages (Kata Kolok, Al-Sayyid Bedouin), Nicaraguan Sign Language and the emergence question. Full languages with their own phonology and syntax.
- **Pidgins and creoles**: Tok Pisin, Bislama, Haitian Creole, Kriol, the substrate and bioprogram debates
- **Constructed languages**: auxiliary (Esperanto, Interlingua, Volapük), engineered (Lojban, Ithkuil), artistic (Quenya and Sindarin, Klingon, Dothraki). Junction with Decipherment via the Voynich hypotheses.
- **Ancient corpus languages**: the practical question of what is actually readable, and how much text survives for each. This is where corpus size, and therefore decipherability, is recorded.
