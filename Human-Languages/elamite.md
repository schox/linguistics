# Elamite

Elamite is the extinct language of southwestern Iran, written in cuneiform and attested from the 24th to the 4th century BCE. It has no established relative, and it is one of the three languages of the Achaemenid royal inscriptions, which is how it enters the decipherment history.

## Classification

Glottolog classifies Elamite as an isolate: a single language, no children, no parent, glottocode `elam1244` and ISO 639-3 `elx`. It is not in WALS, which was checked; the atlas covers no Elamite datapoints, so no typological sketch is available from the instrument used for the living isolates in this subfield.

Grillot-Susini states the position on relatives directly: "The genetic relationship of Elamite to other languages has not so far been established with certainty, though attempts have been made to connect it with Dravidian (McAlpin)." That Elamo-Dravidian proposal is McAlpin's and is named here as a proposal, not adopted; neither McAlpin's argument nor its critics were read for this note.

## Attestation

Elamite is a corpus language, and the corpus is cuneiform. Grillot-Susini records that most texts were found at Susa, with others from sites in western and southwestern Iran and, to the east, in Fārs.

The attested span divides into four periods: Old, Middle, Neo-Elamite, and Late Elamite or Achaemenid. Most surviving material comes from the last three, and the language changed considerably across them, in Grillot-Susini's account especially in its syntax. There are also indications, on the same authority, that the material does not represent a single dialect.

The Achaemenid period is where Elamite meets the [decipherment of cuneiform](../Decipherment/cuneiform.md): the trilingual inscription at [Behistun](../places/behistun.md) carries Old Persian, Elamite and Akkadian, and the vault covers the other two languages as [Old Persian](old-persian.md) and [Akkadian](akkadian.md).

**Elamite is not proto-Elamite.** [Proto-Elamite](../Decipherment/proto-elamite.md) is an undeciphered script of the late fourth millennium BCE, roughly a millennium and a half earlier than the earliest Elamite text described here, and whether it records the Elamite language is precisely what is unknown about it. Linear Elamite is a third thing again. The names invite the confusion and the distinction is the reason this note exists separately.

## Structure

Grillot-Susini describes Elamite as agglutinative. A word consists of a base plus one or more suffixes; the base ends in a vowel, and is either a root already ending in a vowel or a consonant-final root with a vowel added, an addition that can double the root's final consonant. Roots without suffixes, and bases with only vocalic extensions, can function as words. The word classes she names are nouns, pronouns and verbs.

The verb stands at the end of its clause, and the most common order in simple statements is subject-object-verb, which she also frames as agent-patient-verb. Relations inside nominal chains are carried by nominal suffixes and by word order rather than by case in the familiar sense.

The distinctive feature is a system of nominal classes marked by agreement. Grillot-Susini's examples include *u sunki-k*, "I king(-k)", where the animate first-person singular suffix shows that "king" refers to "I", and *nap pahi-r*, "god protector(-r)", where the animate third-person singular suffix shows what "protector" refers to. A neutral class marked *-n* and a class marked *-me* also appear in her worked example.

## Open questions

- **No corpus size is recorded here.** The number of Elamite texts and their distribution across the four periods would be the single most useful figure for this note, since corpus size is the variable the decipherment material turns on, and no source read here states it.
- **Grillot-Susini's own grammar has not been read**, nor Hinz and Koch's dictionary, nor Hallock 1969. Everything above rests on one encyclopedia article, which is thin support for a language with this much bearing on the Decipherment area.
- **The Elamo-Dravidian proposal is named and not assessed.** McAlpin's work was not read, and neither was any reply to it.
- **Whether the Achaemenid Elamite of the inscriptions is a living register or a chancery translation language** is not addressed by the source read, and it bears directly on what the Behistun text can be used for.
