# Egyptian

The language of ancient Egypt, and the reason its script could be read. A branch of Afro-Asiatic with its own single-member branch in Glottolog's classification, attested across more than three thousand years, which makes it one of the longest continuously documented languages in the world.

## Why this note exists in a decipherment vault

Because a script is not a language, and [Egyptian hieroglyphs](../Decipherment/egyptian-hieroglyphs.md) is the case where having the language available is what made the difference.

The vault's undeciphered scripts fail this test in different ways. [Linear A](../Decipherment/linear-a.md) has no identified language. The Indus corpus may not encode one. [Rongorongo](../Decipherment/rongorongo.md) probably has a known language, Old Rapanui, and is still unread. Egyptian is the case where the language was not merely identified but **available in a late form that scholars could already read**, and that is what a bilingual on its own could never have supplied.

## Coptic is the same language, late

Coptic is the final stage of Egyptian, written in an alphabet derived from Greek, and it remained in liturgical use in the Coptic Church long after it ceased to be spoken as a vernacular. That continuity is the substantive fact behind the decipherment.

Dalby records that Arab historians in medieval Egypt were the first to study hieroglyphs by comparing them with the Coptic used by Coptic priests around them, naming Dhul-Nun al-Misri and Ibn Wahshiyya in the ninth and tenth centuries. The insight that unlocked Egyptian was therefore available, and acted on, roughly a millennium before Champollion, who came to it with the advantage of the trilingual and of a European scholarly apparatus. The vault's rule about credit applies here as much as it does to [Alice Kober](../people/alice-kober.md).

## Open questions

- **This note is a stub written to give the script a language to point at.** It has none of what a `Language` note should carry: the stages of the language (Old, Middle, Late Egyptian, Demotic, Coptic), their dates, the writing systems used at each stage, or the grammar. The frontmatter is verified; the body is not a treatment.
- **`era: extinct` is a simplification.** Egyptian has no native speakers, which is what the enum value means, but Coptic's continuing liturgical use makes "extinct" a blunter statement than the case deserves. The vault's enum has no value for a liturgically surviving language.
- **Coptic may warrant its own note**, with its own `Script` note for the Coptic alphabet, ISO 15924 `Copt` 204 and Unicode blocks U+2C80-U+2CFF and U+102E0-U+102FF (Coptic Epact Numbers), both verified. Whether Coptic is a stage of Egyptian or a language in its own right is a real question that decides whether it is one note or two.
