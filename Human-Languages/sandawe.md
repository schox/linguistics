# Sandawe

Sandawe is a living language of north-central Tanzania, and one of two click languages of East Africa with no demonstrated relative, the other being [Hadza](hadza.md).

## Classification

Glottolog classifies Sandawe as an isolate: a single language, no children, no parent, glottocode `sand1273` and ISO 639-3 `sad`. The reference given for family membership is Güldemann 2018, at pages 98 to 107, the same pages cited for Hadza. WALS holds it under the code `sdw`, in a family and genus of its own.

Sandawe was grouped under "Khoisan" on the strength of its clicks, and that label is areal rather than genealogical. It has also been compared specifically to the Khoe languages of southern Africa, which is a narrower proposal than the old Khoisan grouping and a different kind of claim; neither the proposal nor its assessment was read for this note, and the classification above records only that Glottolog treats the language as an isolate.

Glottolog assesses it as threatened, citing Campbell and colleagues 2022, who report it as "Vulnerable (20 percent certain, based on the evidence available)" after Brenzinger 2011. The low certainty is the assessors' own and is worth carrying with the label.

## Structure

The sketch below is drawn from WALS, which codes each value against named grammars; those grammars are named here because the claim is theirs. Coverage is moderate: 58 datapoints, well short of the 149 WALS holds for [Basque](basque.md) but far better than the 13 it holds for [Hadza](hadza.md).

The sound system is large and click-bearing. WALS codes a large consonant inventory, a high consonant-to-vowel ratio, an average vowel quality inventory of five or six, and clicks under the feature for uncommon consonants, on Dempwolff 1916, Elderkin 1982, Kagaya 1993, Maddieson and colleagues 1999 and Tucker and colleagues 1977. On these four values Sandawe and Hadza are coded identically, which is the typological convergence that produced the Khoisan grouping and does not by itself support it.

Sandawe is verb-final, with SOV order, postpositions, genitive before noun and adjective after noun, coded on Eaton 2008. Inflection is strongly suffixing, on Eaton 2008 and van de Kimmenade 1954, and case is marked by postpositional clitics rather than suffixes proper. Negation is affixal and follows the verb.

The value that separates it from the other isolates covered here is the expression of pronominal subjects: WALS codes obligatory pronouns in subject position, on Eaton 2008, where [Basque](basque.md), [Ainu](ainu.md) and [Burushaski](burushaski.md) all mark the subject on the verb and [Nivkh](nivkh.md) makes the pronoun optional.

## Open questions

- **No speaker figure is recorded here.** Ethnologue holds the standard count behind a paywall.
- **The Khoe comparison is named above and not assessed**, because neither its proponents nor its critics were read. It is the live version of the question the discarded Khoisan label used to answer.
- **Whether Sandawe and Hadza are related** is not answered by the sources read here. They are coded alike on every phonological feature WALS holds for both, and that is precisely the evidence that does not settle it.
- **Güldemann 2018 and Eaton 2008 have not been read.** Between them they carry the classification and most of the structure above.
