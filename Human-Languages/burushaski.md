# Burushaski

Burushaski is a living language of the Karakoram, spoken by the Burusho in northern Pakistan and in India. Glottolog records it as shifting: the dominance of Urdu has produced, in the assessment it cites, a strong push for a majority of Burushos to shift to Urdu, with younger speakers often having only receptive proficiency and fluency declining sharply across the second and third generations.

## Classification

Glottolog classifies Burushaski as an isolate, citing Berger 1998, and assigns it the glottocode `buru1296` and the ISO 639-3 code `bsk`. WALS holds it under the code `bur`, in a family and genus of its own.

It appears in both samples WALS defines, the 100-language sample and the 200-language sample. As with [Basque](basque.md), that follows from samples built to maximize genealogical diversity, where a family of one is a branch by itself.

## Attestation

**Not covered here, for want of a source read.** Burushaski has no early written record and its documentation is a modern descriptive tradition rather than a corpus, which is a materially different situation from [Basque](basque.md) and from the ancient isolates in this subfield. Lorimer 1935 and Berger 1998 are the works WALS and Glottolog rest on and neither was read for this note.

## Structure

The sketch below is drawn from WALS, which codes each value against named grammars; those grammars are named here because the claim is theirs.

Burushaski is verb-final, with SOV order, postpositions, genitive before noun and adjective before noun, coded on Berger 1974, Tiffou and Pesot 1989 and Lorimer 1935.

Its sound system is large. WALS codes the consonant inventory as large and the consonant-to-vowel ratio as high, against an average vowel quality inventory of five or six, on Anderson 1997b and Morgenstierne 1945. It is the largest consonant inventory of the four isolates written up together here.

The morphology is richly marked. WALS records eight or nine cases, marked by case suffixes, and inflection as weakly suffixing, on Berger 1998, Tiffou and Pesot 1989 and Lorimer 1935. The distinctive value is gender: Burushaski has four, on Berger 1998 and Lorimer 1935, where the other three isolates covered here have none.

Alignment of case marking of full noun phrases is coded ergative-absolutive, on Berger 1998, and alignment of verbal person marking is coded split, on Tiffou and Pesot 1989. Negation is affixal rather than a separate word, and polar questions are formed by interrogative verb morphology, on Tiffou and Pesot 1989 and Lorimer 1935.

## Open questions

- **No speaker figure is recorded here.** Glottolog's endangerment assessment describes the direction of change without giving a count, and Ethnologue's figure is behind a paywall.
- **Berger 1998 and Lorimer 1935 have not been read.** Nearly everything in the structure section rests on them at one remove, through WALS, and the descriptive tradition they represent has not been checked against more recent work.
- **The proposed external relationships are not covered.** Burushaski has been linked to Yeniseian and to a wider Dene-Caucasian grouping, and those proposals have critics; naming either side needs sources not read here, and the classification above records only that Glottolog treats the language as an isolate.
- **Whether Yasin Burushaski is a separate language or a dialect** is not settled in the sources read. WALS lists Burushaski (Yasin) among its alternative names, which suggests the question exists without answering it.
