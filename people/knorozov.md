# Yuri Knorozov

Soviet and Russian Mesoamericanist (1922-1999), who established that [Mayan hieroglyphs](../Decipherment/maya-script.md) record sounds.

## The argument

Diego de Landa, in the sixteenth century, asked Maya informants for the glyphs corresponding to Spanish letters and wrote down what they gave him. The Maya had no alphabet, so the resulting table did not mean what he took it to mean, and for four centuries it was treated as a muddle.

Knorozov's move, published in 1952, was to read it as a record of **syllables** rather than letters, and therefore as evidence that the glyphs carry phonetic values. He set the system out in *Sistema pis'ma drevnikh maiia* (Moscow: Izdatel'stvo Akademii nauk SSSR, 1955), published with a parallel Spanish title, *La escritura de los antiguos Mayas*.

The document he needed had been in print since the sixteenth century. What was missing was the idea that it was a key, which is the same shape as the [Egyptian](../Decipherment/egyptian-hieroglyphs.md) case, where medieval Arab scholars had already thought to compare hieroglyphs with Coptic.

## Resisted for reasons that were not about evidence

This is why the note matters to the vault.

The University of North Carolina's exhibit puts it in one sentence: "The opposition of leading Mayanist J. Eric S. Thompson, as well Cold War suspicion of Knorozov's Soviet citizenship, slowed the reception of his brilliant analysis for many years."

Thompson dominated the field and held that the script was ideographic. He is reported to have dismissed Knorozov's work as communist propaganda, though the vault has read only his opponents on this and records the characterization as theirs.

Set that against the vault's other blocked decipherments and it is a distinct category:

| Script | What blocked it |
| --- | --- |
| [Egyptian hieroglyphs](../Decipherment/egyptian-hieroglyphs.md) | a wrong theory, Horapollo's symbolic tradition |
| [Cuneiform](../Decipherment/cuneiform.md) | an argument from implausibility, answered by experiment in 1857 |
| Mayan hieroglyphs | the standing of one scholar, and the nationality of another |

Only the last has nothing to do with the corpus or with any claim about writing. It is the case the vault should keep when it is tempted to treat decipherment as a purely evidential matter, and it sits oddly against the [evidentiary threshold](../Decipherment/evidentiary-threshold-and-unicity-distance.md), which explains failures that are genuinely about evidence.

Vindication came from [Proskouriakoff](proskouriakoff.md), Michael Coe, Linda Schele and David Stuart among others, and the modern reading of Maya rests on the principle he stated.

## Open questions

- **Nothing of his has been read.** The 1955 book is in Russian, and the 1952 article is not even identified by title in the source used here.
- **The principle of synharmony is not covered.** His rule for how consonant-vowel-consonant words are written with two syllabic signs, the second's vowel echoing the first's, is central to the method and is stated nowhere in this note because no source read here sets it out properly.
- **Thompson deserves better than a one-line dismissal.** He led the field for decades and the vault has read none of him. Attributing "communist propaganda" to him at second hand, without a citation to his own words, is exactly the sort of thing the vault's rule on contested claims exists to prevent.
- **His biography is almost entirely absent.** The circumstances in which he worked, including whether he ever visited a Maya site, are not covered.
