# Michael Ventris

British architect (12 July 1922 to 6 September 1956) who read [Linear B](../Decipherment/linear-b.md). The most conspicuous absence in this vault until now: it has carried notes on [Alice Kober](alice-kober.md) and [John Chadwick](john-chadwick.md) for months without one on the person who solved it.

## Not a philologist

He was an architect, trained at the Architectural Association School and qualified in 1948, and he worked as one.

The Cambridge Faculty of Classics records that he first saw Linear B in 1936, aged fourteen, when Arthur Evans showed tablets at a British School at Athens exhibition. He published his first article on the script at eighteen, in the *American Journal of Archaeology*.

**That first article argued the language was [Etruscan](../Human-Languages/etruscan.md).** It was wrong, and he held the idea for years.

That is worth more than a biographical note. [Why a script stays unread](../Decipherment/why-scripts-stay-unread.md) argues that the hardest obstacles are frames rather than evidence, and that they are invisible from inside because everyone shares them. Ventris is the case where the person who broke the script had to break his own frame first, publicly, having committed to it in print as a teenager. Whatever the decipherment took, it included abandoning the thing he was known for.

## The method, and what he did with it

In 1950 he sent a questionnaire to Linear B scholars around the world, and from 1951 circulated **Work Notes** to anyone interested, setting out his reasoning as it developed.

That is unusual enough in this vault to state plainly. Every other solved decipherment here came with a priority quarrel: [Young and Champollion](champollion.md), [Rawlinson and Hincks](rawlinson.md), Bauer and Dhorme and Virolleaud. Ventris circulated his working to his competitors while it was still provisional, and when [Chadwick](john-chadwick.md) wrote to him within weeks of the breakthrough he welcomed the collaboration rather than defending the ground.

The grid he built on was [Kober's](alice-kober.md). She had established which signs shared consonants and which shared vowels, from distribution alone and without assigning a value to any of them. Ventris extended that structure and then tested Greek against it.

Progress accelerated in February 1952, and on 1 July 1952 he announced on the BBC Third Programme that the Linear B documents were, as Cambridge puts it, "after all, written in Greek".

## Two architects

He and [Tatiana Proskouriakoff](proskouriakoff.md) were both trained as architects, and neither was a philologist by training. Both produced results that turned on structure and arrangement rather than on meaning: her pattern of dates across consecutive monuments, his grid of sign relationships.

The vault records this as an observation rather than an explanation. Two cases is not a finding, and the obvious counter is that architecture was simply a common training for clever people of a certain class and period. But the coincidence is exact enough to note, and both of them said something about learning to read structure before content.

## Death

He died in a car accident on 6 September 1956, aged 34, shortly before *Documents in [Mycenaean Greek](../Human-Languages/mycenaean-greek.md)* appeared. The vault's other decipherers mostly lived to defend their claims; he had four years.

## Open questions

- **Nothing here comes from his own writing.** The Work Notes, the 1953 paper with Chadwick and *Documents in Mycenaean Greek* are all primary and none has been read. The vault holds [Ventris and Chadwick 1953](../references/ventris-chadwick-1953.md) and [Chadwick 1958](../references/chadwick-1958.md) as References, both unread.
- **The Etruscan hypothesis is stated and not examined.** When he abandoned it, why, and whether he said anything about the experience, is exactly what would make the point above more than an anecdote.
- **The grid is described and not explained.** How Kober's triplets became Ventris's grid is the technical core of the decipherment and the vault covers it in two sentences across three notes.
- **The circumstances of his death are not covered**, and the note takes no position on anything beyond the fact.
- **`viaf` not looked up.**
