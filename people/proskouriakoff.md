# Tatiana Proskouriakoff

American Mayanist (1909-1985), born in Russia, in the United States from 1916, and trained not as an epigrapher but as an architect.

## An architect's finding

She graduated from the Pennsylvania State College School of Architecture in 1930 and joined an expedition to [Piedras Negras](../places/piedras-negras.md) in 1936. The Archaeological Institute of America records a characteristic episode from that first season: her architectural training led her to expect a stairway on one side of a structure, her supervisor doubted it, and excavation proved her right.

The training matters. She came to the inscriptions as someone used to reading buildings and their arrangement, and what she eventually noticed was a property of how monuments were laid out in series rather than of the glyphs themselves.

## The 1960 paper

[Her paper](../references/proskouriakoff-1960.md) showed that dates on consecutive monuments at Piedras Negras fall into discrete sets, each opening with an ascension motif, each with an initial date some years before its first dedicatory date and an inaugural date within it, and that **the intervals span plausible human lifespans**. From that she concluded that the monuments record births, accessions and reigns of individual rulers, and that the figures carved on them are portraits.

She established what Maya texts are *about* without reading them. The vault has now recorded that move three times, and the pattern is worth stating: the first useful step on an undeciphered script is structural, not semantic.

Ian Graham's verdict, quoted by the Archaeological Institute of America: "at one blow, this short paper freed the study of Maya writing from a lengthy stagnation".

## The comparison with Kober, and where it breaks

She belongs beside [Alice Kober](alice-kober.md), and the resemblance is close. Both refused to assign values they could not justify. Both worked from distribution. Both produced a structural result that made someone else's phonetic breakthrough usable.

But the vault should not flatten them into one story, because the receptions differed sharply. Kober died in 1950 before [Linear B](../Decipherment/linear-b.md) was read and has been recovered slowly since. Proskouriakoff's paper was accepted at once and by everyone, including Thompson, and she lived twenty-five years after it.

The contrast is instructive about *why* the vault keeps meeting the credit problem. Kober's obscurity was not caused by her method; it was caused by dying first. Proskouriakoff did the same kind of work, survived to publish it, and was credited. That suggests the recurring pattern in this area is less about structural work being undervalued than about who is alive when the announcement comes.

There is a second asymmetry in the same episode. Proskouriakoff's finding was welcomed while [Knorozov's](knorozov.md) was resisted for years, and the difference was not the quality of the argument.

## Open questions

- **The 1960 paper is unread**, being paywalled, so this note rests on its abstract and on the AIA's biography.
- **Her later work is not covered**, including *Jades from the Cenote of Sacrifice* (1974) and her long service at the Peabody Museum.
- **Her own view of Knorozov is not established here.** The UNC exhibit lists her among those who validated his work, but the vault has read nothing of hers on the subject.
