# Piedras Negras

A ruined Maya city in the Petén, Guatemala, and the site whose monuments gave [Proskouriakoff](../people/proskouriakoff.md) the pattern that showed [Mayan hieroglyphs](../Decipherment/maya-script.md) record history.

## Why the site matters here

Not for what was excavated out of it, and not for how much survived. Piedras Negras matters because its monuments stand **in series**.

Proskouriakoff's argument depends on consecutive stelae forming discrete sets, each opening with an ascension motif, each carrying an initial date several years before its first dedicatory date. That pattern is only visible if you have a run of monuments from one place in a known order. A single stela, however well preserved, shows nothing; the evidence is in the sequence.

So this is a third kind of provenance argument. [Knossos](knossos.md) mattered for volume, [Behistun](behistun.md) for length, and Piedras Negras for **arrangement**. The vault has been treating corpus size as the variable that decides decipherability, and this is a case where the decisive property of the evidence was its internal ordering rather than its quantity.

Proskouriakoff joined an expedition to the site in 1936 and worked on its monuments for around a quarter of a century before publishing the result in 1960.

## Open questions

- **The excavation history is not recorded.** The University of Pennsylvania Museum ran the expeditions Proskouriakoff joined, and the Peabody Museum has published on the site, but neither has been consulted here.
- **The monument corpus is not quantified.** How many stelae, in how many series, is exactly the fact this note's argument rests on, and it is not stated.
- **`pleiades` is not applicable.** Pleiades covers the Greek and Roman world. A Mesoamerican gazetteer with stable identifiers would be the right join, and none was identified. `geonames` is unfilled.
- **The site's later history is not covered**, including the extent of looting, which bears directly on whether the monument sequence survives intact.
