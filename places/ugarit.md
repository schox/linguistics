# Ugarit

A Bronze Age port city on the northern Syrian coast, at the tell of Ras Shamra, with its harbor at Minet el-Beida, ancient Maʾḫadu. Overdue in this vault twice over: it is the sole provenance of the Cypro-Minoan CM3 texts, and the provenance of the entire [Ugaritic](../Decipherment/ugaritic-script.md) corpus.

Wikidata gives coordinates of 35.601944 N, 35.785556 E, and an end date of around 1190 BCE.

## Two scripts, two problems

This is the only site in the vault that is the provenance of both a solved and an unsolved decipherment.

**Ugaritic** was found here in 1929 and read within a year. Thirty signs, a correctly guessed language family, and it fell.

**[Cypro-Minoan](../Decipherment/cypro-minoan.md) CM3** was also found here, entirely: the third subgroup of the Cypro-Minoan corpus comes from Ugarit and nowhere else. It is unread, and its language is unidentified.

The same site, the same period, the same excavations, and two completely different outcomes. Whatever separates them is not provenance, preservation or archaeological attention, because those are held constant. What differs is the number of signs and whether anyone could name the language, which is the argument the [Ugaritic script](../Decipherment/ugaritic-script.md) note makes.

That makes Ugarit a natural control case, and the vault should use it as one.

## The excavations, and a caution about the story

Claude Schaeffer directed the work at Ras Shamra from 1929. He is the same Schaeffer who later ran the long French mission at [Enkomi](enkomi.md) on Cyprus, which is not a coincidence: he went to Cyprus because of the Cypriot material found here.

The CREWS Project notes that Schaeffer's own account of the discoveries has "a romanticised quality", shared by other stories of the early finds at Ras Shamra, including the widely repeated tale of a peasant ploughing at Minet el-Beida striking a stone that turned out to be part of a buried tomb. CREWS also records that the tablets themselves were found by **Mohamed Moursal**, which is the sort of attribution that usually falls out of the retelling.

The vault's rule on credit applies to excavation as much as to decipherment, and this is a cleaner case than most: a named local workman found the material that produced a decipherment, and the story is normally told about the Frenchman who directed the dig.

## Open questions

- **The excavation history is thin.** Start date, seasons, the division between Ras Shamra and Minet el-Beida, and the scale of the tablet finds are not properly recorded here. Widely repeated dates of 9 May 1929 for the first excavation and 14 May for the first tablets came only from a search summary and are not asserted.
- **No per-corpus counts.** How many Ugaritic tablets, and how many CM3 inscriptions, are not stated in any source read.
- **`pleiades` not looked up.** Ugarit ended around 1190 BCE, before the gazetteer's main period, but the site was known to classical geography and may well have a record.
- **The city's destruction around 1190 BCE** sits in the Late Bronze Age collapse, which the vault touches through [Cypro-Minoan](../Decipherment/cypro-minoan.md) and has nothing on.
- **Modern conditions are not addressed.** The site is in Syria and its condition after the civil war is not covered here.
