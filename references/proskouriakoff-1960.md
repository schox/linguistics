# Proskouriakoff 1960

The paper that showed Maya inscriptions record history, published in April 1960 and only 22 pages long.

Its method is why it belongs in this vault. From the publisher's abstract: the inscriptions at [Piedras Negras](../places/piedras-negras.md) form discrete sets on consecutive monuments; each series opens with an "ascension motif" stela; an "initial date" marked by an upended frog glyph precedes the first dedicatory date by several years; an "inaugural date" marked by a "toothache glyph" falls in the first dedicatory period. **The intervals between these dates span reasonable human lifespans**, which is the argument. If the gaps are the length of a life, the records are about lives.

She therefore established what the texts were about without reading a single word of them. That is the same move [Alice Kober](../people/alice-kober.md) made on Linear B, working from distribution while refusing to assign sound values, and the vault has now met it three times.

The abstract also reports similar date patterns at other sites, and analysis of action glyphs and appellatives as historical rather than astronomical.

**Unread, and paywalled.** American Antiquity is on Cambridge Core behind a subscription, and this article was put online in January 2017. Everything above comes from the publisher's abstract, which is unusually detailed and does carry the substance of the argument, but the paper itself is 22 pages and would be worth having properly. Ian Graham's verdict, quoted by the Archaeological Institute of America, is that "at one blow, this short paper freed the study of Maya writing from a lengthy stagnation".
