# Daniels and Bright 1996

The standard reference survey of the world's writing systems, and the volume through which the terms *abjad* and *abugida* entered general use in the typology.

## What it establishes

**Nothing here rests on the book directly, because it has not been read.** It is held because two sources that have been read point at it, and because a typology note that never named it would be misleading about where its vocabulary comes from.

What the sources consulted actually say:

- [Baroni 2011](baroni-2011.md) cites it inline for the term *abjad*, at p. 128, when defining the category.
- Eleanor Dickey's review in the *Bryn Mawr Classical Review* describes the volume as improving on the older tripartite scheme of logography, syllabary and alphabet: "The problem is neatly solved by the addition of two more terms to the classification, *abjad* (for scripts like unvocalised Hebrew in which only consonants are represented) and *abugida*". The review gives the bibliographic details recorded above.

**The coinage is not established here.** Peter T. Daniels is widely credited with introducing both terms, but Dickey's review does not say who introduced them, only that they appear in this volume, and no primary statement of the coinage has been read. Attribute the terms to the volume, not to a person, until that is checked.

## Access

Print, not held, not read. Bibliographic details were taken from Dickey's review rather than from the book or a publisher's record.

**The place of publication is unresolved.** The review gives Oxford University Press without a place that could be confirmed from the retrieved text, and secondary listings give New York. One of the two is wrong and the discrepancy has not been settled, so no place is recorded above.

Two later works would settle the coinage question directly: Daniels' own chapter "Writing Systems" in *The Handbook of Linguistics*, and his 1990 article in the *Journal of the American Oriental Society*. Neither has been reached; the first is paywalled at Wiley.
