# Unicode Standard 17.0, chapter 6

The chapter of the Unicode core specification that sets out the categories of writing system the standard uses, and assigns a category to every script it encodes. Freely published by the Consortium.

## What it establishes

- **Definitions of five categories**, each in the standard's own words: alphabet ("a writing system that consists of letters for the writing of both consonants and vowels"), abjad ("consonant sounds are written but short vowel sounds are typically omitted"), abugida ("each consonant letter carries an inherent vowel, usually /a/"), logosyllabary ("the units of the writing system are used primarily to write words and/or morphemes of words, with some subsidiary usage to represent syllabic sounds per se"), and featural syllabary, applied to Hangul.
- **Table 6-1**, which sorts every encoded script into six rows: Alphabets, Abjads, Abugidas, Logosyllabaries, Simple Syllabaries, Featural Syllabaries. The standard describes it as "an approximate guide, rather than a definitive classification, because of the mix of features seen in many scripts".
- **A warning against the categories it has just supplied**: "one must always be careful not to assume too much about the structure of a writing system from its nominal classification."
- **Composite systems**, with Japanese as the worked case: it "mixes a logosyllabary (Han), two syllabaries (Hiragana and Katakana), and one alphabet (Latin, for romaji)".
- **Classification by use rather than descent**, illustrated by Mahajani and Multani, which derive from Brahmic writing but lack the virama, matras and conjuncts of an abugida and so "behave respectively as an alphabet and an abjad, and are encoded and classified accordingly".
- **The dual sense of "writing system"**, used in the standard both for a category of script and for the way a particular language is written.

The classification is built for encoding rather than for linguistic description, which is the reason to read it alongside a linguistic source rather than instead of one.
