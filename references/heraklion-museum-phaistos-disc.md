# Heraklion Archaeological Museum, the Phaistos Disc

The catalog entry from the museum that holds the object, which makes it the primary record for the physical description even though it is a short web page.

Facts taken from it: discovered by Luigi Pernier in a room of the Palace of Phaistos on 3 July 1908; both faces impressed with seals before firing while the clay was still wet, bearing 241 signs in total; a vertical line with five dots on the outer edge of each face marks where to begin and which way to read, from the rim inward; the signs are arranged in 61 groups, presumed to be words, separated by incised lines.

The museum also offers an interpretation, which the vault reports rather than adopts: that the repetition of certain sign-groups "like a refrain, is the strongest evidence that the inscription is a hymn or incantation". It states that no definitive conclusion has been reached on the contents, or on any relationship to Linear A, Cretan Hieroglyphic and Linear B.
