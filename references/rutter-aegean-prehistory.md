# Rutter, Aegean Prehistoric Archaeology

A Dartmouth College course resource covering the Aegean Bronze Age lesson by lesson with bibliography. Credited to Jeremy B. Rutter, Professor of Classics, with JoAnn Gonzalez-Major as instructional designer. Lesson 10 covers the Aegean scripts.

Used here for the archaeological distribution of the Linear A corpus, which the linguistic literature tends to state only in aggregate. Lesson 10 gives: "Major archives have been found at Ayia Triadha (168 tablets) dating to LM IA and at Chania dating to about the same time period. Tablets are also known from Archanes, Knossos, Mallia, Phaistos, and Zakro." It describes Linear A as a syllabary of "some 85 distinct signs", found at some twenty sites, with true texts outside Crete only at Ayia Irini on Keos, Phylakopi on Melos and Akrotiri on Thera.

Two of those figures need care when set beside the linguistic sources, and both disagreements are recorded in [Linear A](../Decipherment/linear-a.md) rather than resolved. The tablet count for Hagia Triada differs from the figure circulating in tertiary sources, and the dating of that archive to LM IA differs from the LM IB of [Montecchi 2019](montecchi-2019.md). The sign count of 85 is not comparable with the 180 simple signs in [Salgarella and Castellan 2020](salgarella-castellan-2020.md), because the two are counting different things: syllabic values against the full inventory of simple signs.
