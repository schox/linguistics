# Godart and Olivier 1976

Known universally as **GORILA**, from the authors' initials and the title. Five volumes published by the École française d'Athènes between 1976 and 1985, and still the only complete corpus of Linear A inscriptions.

Each document is presented as a photograph, a drawing and two transcriptions, indexed by findspot and support type following the Raison-Pope index. The standard sign list is in volume 5, pp. xxii-xxvii. Unicode's Linear A block order mainly follows GORILA's, so the corpus is upstream of the encoding as well as of the scholarship.

The reason it matters to this vault is not its content but its format. [Salgarella and Castellan 2020](salgarella-castellan-2020.md) built SigLA precisely because a printed edition "does not allow to carry out any statistical and comparative analysis of signs and sign-sequences". A corpus that cannot be counted cannot be tested against, which puts an infrastructure problem directly in the path of the evidentiary argument in [The evidentiary threshold and unicity distance](../Decipherment/evidentiary-threshold-and-unicity-distance.md).

**Correction, 2026-08-10: "print-only" was an overstatement of the source.** Salgarella and Castellan write that GORILA is "solely available in print form", but add in parentheses "although scans have recently been put online by publishers". This vault dropped the parenthesis and asserted the stronger claim. Scans therefore exist; what does not follow is that the corpus is analyzable, since a scan is an image and the barrier SigLA exists to remove is the absence of machine-readable sign data, not the absence of a digital copy. The drawings in GORILA are also reported to be under copyright restrictions, which is why SigLA redrew every document by hand. The substantive point survives; the wording did not.

Salgarella and Castellan also report an addition to GORILA in preparation by Freo and Zurbach.

**Not consulted directly.** The volumes were not available here, in print or as the publishers' scans, so every figure this vault takes from GORILA is taken at second hand through [Petrolito et al. 2015](petrolito-et-al-2015.md) and [Salgarella and Castellan 2020](salgarella-castellan-2020.md), both of which cite it explicitly. The volume-by-volume publication dates given above come from contemporary reviews in *Revue des Études Grecques*, not from the volumes themselves.
