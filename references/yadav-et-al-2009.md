# Yadav et al. 2009

A bigram Markov analysis of the Indus corpus by the same group as [Rao et al. 2009](rao-2009.md), including Mahadevan. Useful here for three things.

**The corpus figures from the pro-linguistic side.** It uses "the electronic concordance of Mahadevan, henceforth referred to as M77, which records 417 unique signs in 3573 lines of 2906 texts", and reports "Around 400 distinct signs have been identified, though some researchers identify up to 676 distinct signs. The total texts number to about 3000." Set beside [Farmer, Sproat and Witzel 2004](farmer-sproat-witzel-2004.md), the counts agree and only the reading of them differs.

**A carefully limited conclusion.** The paper says the script "is a structured sign system showing features of a formal language" but that it "cannot conclusively establish that it encodes natural language". That is a more restrained claim than the [Rao et al. 2009](rao-2009.md) Science paper attracted in press coverage, and it is worth quoting when the entropy debate is described.

**The pruning problem.** Their EBUDS working set drops damaged, illegible, duplicate and multi-line texts, cutting 2,906 texts to 1,548 and losing 40 of the 417 signs entirely. That is a concrete illustration of how a small corpus shrinks further once made analyzable, which is the mechanism behind the evidentiary threshold rather than an aside about data cleaning.

**Version note.** This is the arXiv preprint, v1, stamped 20 January 2009 and carrying "Manuscript received December, 2008". Whether it was subsequently published in a journal, and with what changes, was not established here.
