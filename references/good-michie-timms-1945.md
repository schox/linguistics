# Good, Michie and Timms 1945

The internal Bletchley Park account of how the Lorenz cipher was broken, written in 1945 by three of the people who did it, classified for fifty-five years, and released to the Public Record Office in 2000. A primary document rather than a history of one.

## What it establishes

Not yet drawn on for content. Held because it is the primary source for a subject this area names and has no material on, and because its coverage is documented on the archive's index:

- The German Tunny (Lorenz) machine and its cryptographic properties.
- The breaking methods, with the statistical emphasis the title announces: wheel-breaking, chi-breaking, and machine setting.
- Colossus, described there as "the world's first large-scale electronic digital computer, installed at Bletchley Park in December 1943", and the earlier Robinson counting machines.
- The organization of the codebreaking operation, and the progression from hand methods to machine methods.

The statistical emphasis is the reason it matters beyond its own subject. The methods it records are the same apparatus that the Decipherment area borrows, applied under wartime conditions to an adversarial system rather than to an accidental one.
