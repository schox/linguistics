# Petrolito et al. 2015

Describes the construction of a TEI-EpiDoc digital corpus of the Linear A transcriptions, and in passing gives the clearest citable statement of corpus size in the literature the vault has reached.

The figure this vault relies on, from section 2.1: "There are 1,427 Linear A documents containing 7,362-7,396 signs, much less than the quantity of data we have for Linear B (more than 4,600 documents containing 57,398 signs)". The paper attributes both counts to Younger.

Also useful for the survey of decipherment hypotheses in its introduction, each attributed: Greek-like (Nagy 1963), a distinct Indo-European branch (Owens 1999), Anatolian and close to Luwian (Palmer 1958), an archaic form of Phoenician (Dietrich and Loretz 2001), Indo-Iranian (Faure 1998), and Etruscan-like (Facchetti and Negri 2003). The paper reports these as positions held, not as findings.

Its Table 1 gives Linear A a time span of 2500-1450 BC, which is markedly earlier than the range in [Salgarella and Castellan 2020](salgarella-castellan-2020.md) and than the archaeological phasing in [Rutter, Aegean Prehistoric Archaeology](rutter-aegean-prehistory.md). Treat the start date as an outlier rather than as settled.
