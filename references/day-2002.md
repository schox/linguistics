# Day 2002

An article whose entire purpose is to establish who did what in the Ugaritic decipherment, by Peggy L. Day of the University of Winnipeg. Open access through the CSIC's SEL site.

Its opening states the problem: three scholars are generally credited with major roles, Hans Bauer, Paul Dhorme and Charles Virolleaud, and only Alan D. Corré had previously analyzed their precise contributions. Day judges Corré's reconstruction "in general, commendably sound" but incomplete, and rebuilds the sequence from paper presentation dates, article completion dates, private communications and intellectual context.

That method is the reason the article is useful here. Decipherment credit is usually argued from publications, and publications lag the work. Day's finding that lecture dates and letters change the picture is a general caution, not one about Ugaritic.

Two things taken from it into the vault:

**The `grzn` confirmation.** Dussaud reported that Bauer's results were verified when, following Virolleaud's suggestion to look for a four-consonant word meaning "axe", Bauer's system yielded `grzn`, which has a Hebrew cognate of that meaning. A prediction made before the reading and satisfied by it.

**The priority tangle.** After the Académie meeting of 24 October, the French press reported Virolleaud's success as though no one else had made progress. Dhorme, who had known nothing of Virolleaud's work until he read the releases, knew that to be untrue. Dhorme went on to produce a revised alphabet by 8 December 1930, proposing values for 26 signs "with appropriate credit to Bauer".
