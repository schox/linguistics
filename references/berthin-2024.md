# Berthin 2024

An authenticity study of six rongorongo objects, testing them against provenance, production technique, glyph vocabulary, conformity to Zipf's law, internal verse pattern and object-specific features.

Used in [Rongorongo](../Decipherment/rongorongo.md) for the corpus figures, which it states cleanly: about 120 unique base characters, which combine into hundreds of compound forms; authentic material on 25 or 26 wooden slabs or objects; and "The RR corpus comprises approximately 14,000 signs."

Two things make it more useful than a corpus edition would be on its own.

**It treats authenticity as a live variable.** Objects carrying rongorongo-like glyphs have been made for sale to visitors since the nineteenth century and still are, so the boundary of the corpus is not settled. Berthin notes that forgeries "might be innocently imitated or purposefully deceiving", and makes the sharp observation that portions of imitations which quote directly from authentic but now-lost texts should be *added* to the validated corpus rather than discarded. That is a problem none of the vault's other scripts has in this form.

**It records how the reading community ended.** Citing Fischer, it states that most rongorongo scribes and cantors were kidnapped or died in the genocide and disease that accompanied the colonization of Rapa Nui, and that by June 1869, when Bishop Tepano Jaussen brought the script to notice, few interpreters are presumed to have remained alive. See the note on [Rapa Nui](../places/rapa-nui.md).

Also gives the reading order (inverse boustrophedon, starting at the lower left with the object turned 180 degrees at each line end) and summarizes Barthel's 1958 classification, whose numbers run from 1 to 799.
