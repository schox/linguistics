# Englund 2004

Chapter 5 of Houston's *The First Writing*, by the scholar most associated with the proto-Elamite corpus, and made freely available by the Cuneiform Digital Library Initiative, which he co-founded.

This is the source the vault should have had for [Proto-Elamite](../Decipherment/proto-elamite.md) from the start. It supplies figures, a sign-list history, findspots, and an unusually direct assessment of why the field is where it is.

**Corpus.** "The proto-Elamite corpus numbers just over 1,600 pieces, with around 10,000 lines of text, that is, about a quarter as many as from Babylonia (still, it represents a large amount of material compared to the relatively humble inscriptions of Linear A or of early Harappan)."

That parenthesis is worth the whole chapter for this vault's purposes. Englund is comparing his own corpus to [Linear A](../Decipherment/linear-a.md) and the [Indus script](../Decipherment/indus-script.md) unprompted, and putting proto-Elamite well above both.

**Sign lists.** Mecquenem's final list of 1949 ran to "upwards of 5,500 signs" and contained large numbers of variants; Meriggi (1971-1974) grouped presumed variants under discrete headings "and so arrived at a total of less than 400". A fourteenfold spread on one corpus, which is the widest the vault has recorded anywhere.

**On the field.** The publication of tablets "appears to have proceeded with little understanding of the text corpus and the accounting system it represented, and with little attention paid to an accurate representation in hand copies of the texts themselves", and the texts "have played an historically minor role relative to early cuneiform".

**Comparanda.** Proto-cuneiform is given as over 6,000 tablets and fragments with more than 38,000 lines, after some 400 new texts reached the London market from post-Kuwait-War Iraq.

His map (Fig. 5.2) marks sites with proto-Elamite tablets. Read from the figure rather than from prose, they appear to be Susa, Sialk, Malyan, Tepe Yahya, Shahr-i Sokhta and Ozbaki.

**Read in part.** Pages 100-103 were read directly; the chapter runs to 149 and the rest has not been. The figures above are from those opening pages.
