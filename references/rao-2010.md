# Rao et al. 2010

The published reply to [Sproat 2010](sproat-2010.md), in the following issue of the same journal. Together the two are the citable form of the Indus entropy dispute, which is otherwise usually cited to blog posts and personal pages.

The position taken on the author's summary page is that the argument is Bayesian rather than demonstrative: conditional entropy is offered as evidence favoring the linguistic hypothesis when combined with other properties of the corpus, not as proof. The summary page also makes the "absence of evidence is not evidence of absence" reply to the missing-long-texts argument, holding that long texts may have been written on perishable materials.

Worth noting for the vault's purposes: this reply disputes the **interpretation** of the corpus statistics, not the statistics. Both sides of the dispute work from [Mahadevan 1977](mahadevan-1977.md) and report compatible counts.

**Unread.** The characterization above comes from the authors' own summary page, not from the article. The article itself is behind the MIT Press paywall; an author copy appears at the open-access link.
