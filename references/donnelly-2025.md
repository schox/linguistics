# Donnelly 2025

A single-author Element on Cypro-Minoan by Cassandra M. Donnelly of the University of Cyprus. Published online 1 December 2024, in print 2 January 2025. Online ISBN 9781009381840.

Found while chasing the other two Elements, and it matters more than a bibliographic addition should, because its abstract supplies a causal account of something [Cypro-Minoan](../Decipherment/cypro-minoan.md) currently only describes.

That note records a corpus of 243 short inscriptions scattered across Cyprus, Ugarit and elsewhere, and treats the shortness as a brute fact. Donnelly's framing, quoted from the abstract, is that Cypro-Minoan has "few clay tablets, characteristic of neighboring scribal-based, administrative writing traditions. Instead, Cypro-Minoan writers wrote on mercantile objects, outside of scribal schools."

If that holds, the corpus is short and scattered because of *who was writing and why*, not because of what happened to survive. That is a different explanation from the one the vault applies to Linear A and the Indus script, where the constraint is preservation and excavation. It would make Cypro-Minoan a case where the writing practice itself, mercantile rather than palatial, set the evidentiary limit at the moment of inscription.

The abstract also notes that Cypro-Minoan remained in use after the administrative collapse of c. 1177 BCE took the palatial writing systems with it, "presaging the spread of the Phoenician alphabet".

**Unread, and paywalled.** Not open access; Cambridge Core requires a login. Everything above is from the abstract page. The claim summarized here is consequential enough for [Cypro-Minoan](../Decipherment/cypro-minoan.md) that it should be read before the note is rewritten around it, and it is currently recorded there as a reported position rather than adopted.
