# Mahadevan 1977

The standard concordance of Indus inscriptions, known in the literature as **M77**. Every corpus statistic in [Indus script](../Decipherment/indus-script.md) traces back to it.

Its authority is best shown by who uses it. [Farmer, Sproat and Witzel 2004](farmer-sproat-witzel-2004.md) draw their sign-frequency case from it while arguing the corpus is not writing at all; [Yadav et al. 2009](yadav-et-al-2009.md) build their n-gram models on the electronic version of it while arguing the opposite, with Mahadevan himself as a co-author. The two camps disagree about almost everything except the counts, and the counts are these.

The figures the two sides report from it agree to within rounding: 417 distinct signs; 2,905 or 2,906 texts; 13,372 sign occurrences; 3,573 lines.

**Not consulted directly.** An 829-page print memoir. Everything this vault takes from M77 is taken at second hand from papers that used it, and each is attributed to the paper that reported it rather than to Mahadevan. A scanned copy appears to exist on the Internet Archive; it was not verified as complete or authorized, so it is not linked here as a source.
