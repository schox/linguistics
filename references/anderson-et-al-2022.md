# Anderson et al. 2022

An open-access introductory linguistics textbook, and the backbone the vault uses for the **[Levels of analysis](../General-Linguistics/levels-of-analysis.md)** subfield.

It matters here for a practical reason. General Linguistics is the area this vault leans on hardest and has written least, and most of the standard reference works for it are paywalled. This one is not, which means a claim made in a reference entry can be checked by anyone reading the repository rather than taken on trust.

## What it is

Published by eCampusOntario in 2022 as an Open Educational Resource, by seven authors across McMaster, Queen's and Toronto, with additional chapters contributed by named Indigenous-language speakers and teachers. Licensed **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**.

The non-commercial clause is worth noting because this repository is public. The vault quotes and cites; it does not reproduce the text, and nothing has been copied in bulk. See `_data-sources.md`.

Chapter coverage, from the table of contents:

| Chapters | Subject |
| --- | --- |
| 1-2 | language science, grammars, standards, harm, chatbots |
| 3-4 | phonetics, phonology |
| 5-6 | morphology, syntax |
| 7-8 | semantics, pragmatics |
| 9 | Indigenous languages of Canada, by speakers and teachers |
| 10 | variationist sociolinguistics |
| 11-13 | first and later language acquisition, psycholinguistics |
| 14 | historical linguistics and reconstruction |

It treats **signed languages throughout rather than in an appendix**: signed-language articulators and notation sit inside the phonetics chapter, and signed phonology inside the phonology chapter. That is unusual for an introductory text and is one reason the vault chose it.

## What has actually been read

**Read in part**, and the note should not claim more. The sections consulted so far are 3.6 (segmentation, transcription and the IPA), 4.1 (phonemes and allophones), 4.3 (contrastive distribution and minimal pairs), 5.1 (what is morphology, what is a word), 6.1 (grammaticality judgements), 6.3 (phrases, heads and selection), 7.1 (linguistic meaning), 7.3 (entailments and implicatures), 8.5 (the Cooperative Principle) and 8.9 (illocutionary meaning).

Everything in the six entries under [Levels of analysis](../General-Linguistics/levels-of-analysis.md) descends from those ten sections, together with the chapter and section titles, which are used to say what each subject covers without claiming to have read it. Chapters 9 to 14 are unread.

## A caveat about scope

It is an introductory textbook and is written as one. It is a sound source for what the field's core terms mean and how the field states them, and it is **not** a source for where the field disagrees with itself. Where this vault needs a controversy attributed, it needs something else.

Its examples are also heavily English and North American, and it says so. Claims about what is typologically usual should come from [WALS 2013](wals-2013.md) rather than from here.
