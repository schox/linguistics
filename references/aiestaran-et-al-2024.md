# Aiestaran et al. 2024

The excavation report and epigraphic study of the Hand of Irulegi, a inscribed bronze hand recovered in 2021 from an Iron Age settlement in the Ebro Valley, in present-day Navarre. Open access under a Creative Commons Attribution license, which makes it directly citable rather than reachable only in summary.

## What it establishes

**A dated artifact.** The authors place the inscription in the first century BC. The radiocarbon determinations they report span 146 BC to AD 67 and 334 to 2 BC.

**A described text.** Four lines, in which the authors identify eighteen distinct signs and read words including *sorioneku*, *kunekebe*, *ekiŕateŕen*, *oTiŕtan*, *eseakaŕi* and *eŕaukon*.

**A script claim and a language claim, which are separate.** The script is a Palaeohispanic semi-syllabary, and the authors argue it constitutes a distinct sub-system rather than the Celtiberian one. The language, they argue, is Vasconic, sharing its roots with modern Basque, and the text is on their account "the first example of Vasconic epigraphy".

**A revision of the premise it was found under.** The paper opens by recording the prior assumption it unsettles: few examples of Palaeohispanic writing had been recovered from Vasconic territories, "leading to the assumption that the Vascones were a pre-literate society".

## How it is used here

For the attestation section of [Basque](../Human-Languages/basque.md). The claim that the language is Vasconic is the authors' and is attributed to them in that note rather than stated flat, since a reading of six words from a single object is a narrow evidential base and the paper is recent.
