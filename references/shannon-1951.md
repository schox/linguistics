# Shannon 1951

The companion to [Shannon 1949](shannon-1949.md), and the paper that puts a number on the thing the 1949 paper divides by.

It estimates the entropy of printed English by exploiting the statistical knowledge native speakers already have: subjects are asked to predict the next letter given the preceding text, and the accuracy of their guesses bounds the entropy from above and below.

Figures used in [redundancy](../General-Linguistics/redundancy.md), verified against the scan:

- On short-range statistics, up to 8 letters of context, "the entropy is roughly 2.5 bits per letter" and the redundancy "roughly 50 per cent".
- On long-range statistics, up to 100 letters, the entropy is "of the order of one bit per letter" and the redundancy "roughly 75%".
- For 100 letters of context over a 27-character alphabet including the space, his experimental bounds are an upper estimate of 1.3 and a lower of 0.6 bits per letter.

He defines redundancy as measuring "the amount of constraint imposed on a text in the language due to its statistical structure".

**Read in part.** The figures above and the definition were read from the Internet Archive scan of the issue. The experimental design and the prediction tables were not worked through, and the vault should not represent this as a full reading.
