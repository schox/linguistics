# Beinecke MS 408

The catalog record from the library that holds the Voynich manuscript, and therefore the primary description of the object.

Physical description as given: "ff. 102 (foliation, s. xvi, Arabic numerals; not every leaf foliated) + i (paper)", including 5 double-folio, 3 triple-folio, 1 quadruple-folio and 1 sextuple-folio folding leaves; 225 x 160 mm; parchment; a binding of the eighteenth or nineteenth century in a vellum case. Almost every page carries drawings in ink with washes in green, brown, yellow, blue and red.

The text is described as "in cipher, apparently based on Roman minuscule characters", in an unidentified language, and the content as a "Scientific or magical text".

**The catalog's dating conflicts with the radiocarbon result, and the vault records both.** The record gives "Central Europe [?], s. XV ex-XVI [?]", that is, late fifteenth to sixteenth century, and reports the argument that identification of New World plant specimens would mean the manuscript "could not have been written before 1493". Radiocarbon dating of the parchment in 2009 put it at 1404-1438. The disagreement is not mysterious, since a manuscript catalog of this kind predates the test, but it is unresolved on the page as it stands and a reader who takes the catalog date at face value will be misled.

Provenance as given: owned by Rudolph II, who paid 600 gold ducats, possibly acquired from John Dee; apparently given by Rudolph to Jacobus Horcicky de Tepenecz; presented by Johannes Marcus Marci to Athanasius Kircher in 1666; acquired by Wilfrid Voynich in 1912 from the Jesuit College at Frascati; given to the Beinecke by H. P. Kraus in 1969.

`year` is a proxy, the year of access, since the record carries no visible date of compilation or revision.
