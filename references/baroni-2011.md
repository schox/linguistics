# Baroni 2011

An open-access article arguing that the supposed superiority of the alphabet over syllabic and logosyllabic writing is an ethnocentric prejudice, and that the optimality of a writing system has to be measured against several criteria rather than fidelity of sound mapping alone.

## What it establishes

The article opens with a section of definitions, and that section is what it is drawn on for here rather than the optimality argument that follows it.

- **The cenemic and pleremic distinction**, credited to Coulmas 1989: 38-39. A system is cenemic if its elements stand for units of phonic expression, pleremic if they stand for units of content. Baroni adds that "there is no pure writing system: in every tradition cenemic and pleremic components coexist, to different extents" (p. 128).
- **Definitions of the cenemic categories** at p. 128: syllabary, abjad, abugida, alphabet, and featural writing. The abjad entry carries an inline citation to Daniels and Bright 1996.
- **The three-level distinction** between writing system, script and orthography (pp. 128-129), with the example that Latin and Greek "had the same writing system (an alphabet) but employed different scripts".
- **Transparent and opaque orthographies** (p. 129), with Finnish, Italian, Spanish, Turkish and Georgian given as transparent and French, English, Danish, Khmer and Chinese as opaque. This is the material for a future note on orthographic depth.
- **The alphabetocentrism debate, with both sides attributed**: Gelb 1963: 240 for the teleological view of writing evolving toward the alphabet, Diringer 1948 and Ong 1986 in agreement, against Venezky 1970: 120, Gleason 1961: 419, Coulmas 2009a: 105 and Daniels 1992: 83. Baroni quotes each.

## Access

Open access. The PDF is served directly by the journal and was read from disk after the fetch, since the file is a PDF rather than a web page.

Read to p. 132 of pp. 127-159, which covers the definitions and the opening of the optimality argument. The remaining sections, on criteria for judging optimality and on writing in digital media, have not been read, so nothing here rests on them.

The journal appears under two names. The PDF footer gives *Rivista di Linguistica* 23.2 (2011), and the publisher's domain is `italian-journal-linguistics.com`. Both name the same journal. No DOI is printed on the article.
