# Phonology

The level at which a language's physical signal becomes a system of contrasts. [Anderson et al.](../references/anderson-et-al-2022.md) define it as the study of the underlying patterns that determine which combinations of physical units are valid, and what changes occur when those units are combined.

**This is the level decipherment works at**, and the vault has been assuming it for months without saying so.

## Not a fact about sound

Their opening example of a phonological pattern is not a sound at all. In American Sign Language the signs FOOD and BED compound to form HOME, and the result is not FOOD followed by BED: the two merge into a single sign carrying properties of both, keeping one handshape, one location, reduced repetition, and dropping the head tilt.

Phonology is therefore about the combinatorics of a signal, not about acoustics. The vault should keep this in view, because it is the reason the same analytic vocabulary applies to a script at all.

Their spoken example makes the other half of the point. From the three phones of *meek*, one further combination, [kim], could be an English word and simply is not; the remaining four are unpronounceable for English speakers. **The gap between "impossible" and "possible but unattested" is the whole subject.** A corpus tells you what occurred. Phonology is a claim about what could have.

## Phonemes and allophones

The central abstraction. In most North American English the consonant in the middle of *atom* is an alveolar flap, and in *atomic* it is an aspirated voiceless alveolar plosive. The two are physically different and the words are transparently related, so it is useful to treat the two phones as one object at an abstract level.

That object is a **phoneme**; its physical realizations are **allophones**; the conditions selecting one over another are **environments**. Phonemes are written between slashes, phones between square brackets. Anderson et al. describe a phoneme as a set of allophones, each tied to particular positions.

Their caution is worth carrying: real environments "can be more complex than what is presented in the simpler cases discussed in this textbook".

## How the field decides, and why a decipherer cannot

Two phones **contrast** when their distributions overlap, and a **minimal pair** proves it: two words alike in every position but one. English *pill* and *kill* establish that [p] and [k] contrast. One such pair is sufficient.

Where no minimal pair exists, the field falls back. **Near-minimal pairs** differ elsewhere too, and Anderson et al. are explicit that a single one proves nothing, because the incidental difference may not be incidental; several are needed. **Nonce words** are the third tool: invent the missing member of the pair and ask a speaker whether it could be a different word of the language.

Now read that procedure against the vault's subject matter.

**This is the vault's own reasoning and is not drawn from the source.** All three tools require something a decipherer does not have. A minimal pair is only a minimal pair if the two forms *mean different things*, which presupposes knowing the meanings. A near-minimal pair requires the same judgment about which differences are incidental. A nonce word requires a living speaker willing to say whether it could be a word.

For every language in this vault's Decipherment area there is no speaker, and the meanings are precisely what is being sought. **The standard method of phonemic analysis is unavailable for exactly the cases the vault cares about**, and this is a sharper constraint than the corpus-size argument in [why a script stays unread](../Decipherment/why-scripts-stay-unread.md), because no quantity of text supplies it.

## What Kober did instead

[Alice Kober](../people/alice-kober.md) established, from [Linear B](../Decipherment/linear-b.md) distribution alone, which signs shared a consonant and which shared a vowel, without assigning a value to any of them.

That is a phonological result reached without phonetics and without meanings. She recovered the *shape* of a contrast system, leaving the contrasts unlabeled, and Ventris then tested a language against the shape.

The vault should state the generalization plainly, because it recurs: **decipherment recovers structure before content, and structure is what phonology is.** The grid is a phonological object. So is the abjad-abugida-syllabary typology the vault's eleven `Script` notes all carry in `class`, which is a claim about which phonological units a script's signs correspond to. That typology is used everywhere in this vault and defined nowhere in it.

## Open questions

- **The `class` typology used by every `Script` note is undefined in the vault.** Alphabet, abjad, abugida, syllabary, logographic and mixed are all claims about the phonological unit a sign encodes, and there is no note saying so. This is the most load-bearing gap the starter notes have exposed and belongs under **Writing systems** rather than here.
- **Phonological rules and derivations are not covered**, though the source has four sections on them. Rule notation, natural classes, phonotactics and rule ordering are all absent, and phonotactics in particular is what constrains the possible-word question above.
- **Whether "phonology" is the right word for what a decipherer recovers is not settled here.** Kober's grid is a system of contrasts over signs, and whether that is the language's phonology, the script's, or something in between is the kind of question the vault should not answer by assertion.
- **Signed-language phonology is named and not written**, and it is the case that best justifies treating phonology as modality-independent.
- **Nothing here is sourced from the decipherment side.** The claim that minimal pairs are unavailable to a decipherer is the vault's own and is not attributed to anyone who has argued it.
